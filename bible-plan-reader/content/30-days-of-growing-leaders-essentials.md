## Introduction & How to Use This Guide

Welcome to **Growing Leaders (Essentials Edition)**. 

Whether you are stepping into Christian leadership for the first time, directing a team, serving in your church, or desiring to grow in spiritual maturity in your daily workplace and family, this guide is designed to anchor your leadership in the character, wisdom, and heart of Jesus Christ.

### The 4 Foundations of Christian Leadership
In God's Kingdom, leadership grows across four vital areas:
1. **HEAD (Biblical Mindset)**: Thinking with the truth of the Bible rather than the world's ideas about power.
2. **HEART (Spiritual Character)**: Letting the Holy Spirit heal your insecurities, purify your motives, and build love.
3. **HANDS (Practical Skills)**: Growing in how you communicate, make decisions, handle conflict, and help others succeed.
4. **HABITS (Daily Rhythms)**: Practicing simple daily routines that give you strength and keep you from burning out.

### Daily Structure
Each of the 30 lessons includes:
- **Scripture Reading**: Key Bible verses in the clear, conversational New Living Translation (NLT).
- **Historical & Cultural Context**: A simple look at what the passage meant in its original Bible setting.
- **Devotional Reading**: Practical, real-world wisdom connecting Bible truth to your daily leadership challenges.
- **Questions to Think About**: 3 reflection questions for journaling or small group discussion.
- **Action Step**: A simple, practical exercise to put truth into practice immediately.
- **Prayer**: A direct, personal prayer to talk with God.
- **Review Checkpoint**: At the end of every 5 lessons (Day 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, and 30) to pause, track your growth, and lock in core habits.

May the Lord richly bless, encourage, and guide you as you take this 30-lesson journey!

## Lesson 1: You Don't Start With a Blank Slate

### 1. Scripture Reading
**Genesis 50:15–21 (NLT)**
> <sup>15</sup> But now that their father was dead, Joseph’s brothers became fearful. “Now Joseph will show his anger and pay us back for all the evil we did to him,” they said. <sup>16</sup> So they sent this message to Joseph: “Before your father died, he instructed us <sup>17</sup> to say to you: ‘Please forgive your brothers for the great wrong they did to you—for their sin in treating you so cruelly.’ So we, the servants of the God of your father, beg you to forgive us.” When Joseph received the message, he broke down and wept. <sup>18</sup> Then his brothers came and threw themselves down before him. “Look, we are your slaves!” they said.
>
> <sup>19</sup> But Joseph told them, “Don’t be afraid. Am I God, that I can punish you? <sup>20</sup> You intended to harm me, but God intended it all for good. He brought me to this position so I could save the lives of many people. <sup>21</sup> No, don’t be afraid. I will continue to care for you and your children.” So he reassured them by speaking kindly to them.

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In Bible times, when a family patriarch died, old family fights often turned violent. If someone had wronged you, the culture expected you to get revenge as soon as the father was no longer around to protect them.

Joseph was the second most powerful ruler in all of Egypt. He had the authority to put his brothers in prison or have them executed on the spot. His brothers were so terrified that they fell to the floor and offered to be his slaves. But Joseph said something unexpected: *“Am I God, that I can punish you?”* Instead of using his power to pay them back, Joseph chose to see God’s hand in his story and used his authority to protect and care for them.

### 3. Understanding Your Story
When you step up to lead—whether at church, at work, in school, or at home—you never start with a blank page. You bring your whole life story with you.

Your family, your culture, your childhood memories, your past heartbreaks, and your big wins have all shaped how you act today. Without even realizing it, you have learned default habits about:
- How you handle conflict (Do you blow up, stay silent, or talk it out?)
- How easily you trust others
- What you do when you feel insecure

Many people think leadership only starts when you get a title or job. But God has been shaping your story from day one. When we ignore our past, we often repeat unhealthy habits:
- We try too hard to please people because we crave approval.
- We avoid hard conversations because conflict felt scary growing up.
- We try to control everything because someone let us down in the past.

Joseph went through terrible pain—betrayal, slavery, and wrongful imprisonment. He did not pretend the pain never happened. He said plainly, *"You intended to harm me."* But he also saw that God was bigger than his pain. Because Joseph trusted God's plan, he did not use his power to hurt others. He used it to help them.

When you bring your real story to God, He heals your old wounds and turns your life experience into wisdom to serve others.

### 4. Questions to Think About
1. When there is conflict on your team or in your family, what is your first reaction (shut down, take control, argue, or please others)? Where did you first learn that habit?
2. Joseph chose not to get revenge because he trusted God was in control. How does his story help you look at a painful season in your life?
3. What is one positive strength or gift your life story has given you?

### 5. Action Step
**Your Story Map**: Write down three big moments from your past (such as a family memory, a good teacher/mentor, or a painful disappointment). Beside each one, write one sentence: *"How does this moment affect the way I treat people or make decisions today?"*

### 6. Prayer
*Heavenly Father, thank You that my life is not an accident. Help me to understand the story and habits I bring into leadership. Heal the hurts that make me feel fearful, defensive, or controlling. Give me eyes like Joseph to see that You can turn painful seasons into good. Teach me to lead with humility and grace. In Jesus' name, Amen.*

## Lesson 2: Naming the Good, the Hard, and the Broken

### 1. Scripture Reading
**Psalm 139:13–16, 23–24 (NLT)**
> <sup>13</sup> You made all the delicate, inner parts of my body and knit me together in my mother’s womb. <sup>14</sup> Thank you for making me so wonderfully complex! Your workmanship is marvelous—how well I know it. <sup>15</sup> You watched me as I was being formed in utter seclusion, as I was woven together in the dark of the womb. <sup>16</sup> You saw me before I was born. Every day of my life was recorded in your book. Every moment was laid out before a single day had passed...
>
> <sup>23</sup> Search me, O God, and know my heart; test me and know my anxious thoughts. <sup>24</sup> Point out anything in me that offends you, and lead me along the path of everlasting life.

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In the ancient world, kings put up huge stone monuments and wrote songs bragging about how strong, perfect, and fearless they were. Rulers never admitted weakness or mistakes because they thought it made them look weak.

King David did the exact opposite. Even though he was a great king and warrior, he wrote songs like Psalm 139 inviting God to search his deepest thoughts, worries, and hidden flaws. David knew that true leadership does not come from pretending to be invincible. It comes from being completely honest before God.

### 3. Being Honest Before God
Growing as a leader does not mean pretending your life is perfect. It also does not mean feeling hopeless about your flaws. It begins with simple honesty:
- **Thank God** for the good gifts, talents, and mentors He gave you.
- **Be honest about the hurts**, losses, and disappointments you have experienced.
- **Spot the unhealthy self-defense habits** you built to protect yourself from getting hurt.

Many leaders put on a mask. We think, *"If people see my doubts, fears, or mistakes, they won't respect me."* But hidden fears always leak out. If we don't let God heal our hearts, we will end up using our leadership position to protect our pride, demand attention, or snap at people who criticize us.

David welcomed God's examination. He prayed, *"Search me, O God... know my anxious thoughts."* 

God does not search your heart to embarrass you or put you down. He searches you like a loving doctor who finds the hurt so He can heal it. When you let God love and heal you in secret, you won't need to put on a fake show in public.

### 4. Questions to Think About
1. Why do leaders today often feel like they must pretend they have no fears or weaknesses?
2. In what area of your life or work do you try to look "perfect" so others won't see your struggles?
3. What is one hurt or worry you need to bring honestly to God instead of burying it under busyness?

### 5. Action Step
**A Quiet Check-In**: Take 5 quiet minutes today with a notebook. Read Psalm 139:23–24 out loud. Ask God: *"Lord, is there any hidden pride, anger, or worry in my heart right now?"* Write down what comes to mind and hand it over to Him.

### 6. Prayer
*Lord, You know everything about me. You formed me, and You see all my days. Search my heart today. Show me where I am acting out of fear, pride, or old habits. Thank You that You love me completely, even with my flaws. Heal my wounds and lead me on the right path. In Jesus' name, Amen.*

## Lesson 3: Breaking Unconscious Cycles of Fear

### 1. Scripture Reading
**Exodus 3:10–12 (NLT)**
> <sup>10</sup> “Now go, for I am sending you to Pharaoh. You must lead my people Israel out of Egypt.” <sup>11</sup> But Moses protested to God, “Who am I to appear before Pharaoh? Who am I to lead the people of Israel out of Egypt?” <sup>12</sup> God answered, “I will be with you. And this is your sign that I am the one who has sent you: When you have brought the people out of Egypt, you will worship God at this very mountain.”

**Exodus 4:10–12 (NLT)**
> <sup>10</sup> But Moses pleaded with the LORD, “O Lord, I’m not very good with words. I never have been, and I’m not even now, even though you have spoken to me. I get tongue-tied, and my words get tangled.” <sup>11</sup> Then the LORD asked Moses, “Who makes a person’s mouth? Who decides whether people speak or do not speak, hear or do not hear, see or do not see? Is it not I, the LORD? <sup>12</sup> Now go! I will be with you as you speak, and I will instruct you in what to say.”

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In the ancient world, royal messengers were expected to be powerful public speakers. Moses had grown up in Pharaoh's royal palace, so he knew how grand the Egyptian court was and how terrifying Pharaoh could be.

After spending 40 years in the desert taking care of sheep, Moses felt completely unready to face the most powerful king on earth. In those days, a messenger’s power did not come from himself—it came from the king who sent him. When Moses complained that he was not a good speaker, God reminded him of a simple truth: Moses didn't need to rely on his own eloquence because the Almighty God was going with him.

### 3. Moving Past the Fear of Not Being Enough
Many leaders are stopped by a quiet fear inside: *"If people find out my weaknesses or past failures, they won't follow me."*

Moses spent 40 quiet years in the wilderness. When God called him to lead, Moses brought up every reason why he wasn't good enough:
- Lack of self-worth (*"Who am I?"*)
- Fear that people wouldn't believe him (*"What if they don't listen?"*)
- Lack of speaking ability (*"I get tongue-tied."*)

Look at how God answered. God didn't argue about Moses' weaknesses. He simply pointed Moses to His own power: *"Who makes a person's mouth? Is it not I, the Lord?... I will be with you."*

When we let feelings of inadequacy control us, we usually react in one of two ways:
1. **Shrinking Back**: We make excuses, hesitate, and avoid stepping up when God asks us to serve.
2. **Over-Controlling**: We panic, work ourselves to exhaustion trying to prove we are capable, and get defensive whenever someone questions us.

God does not call the qualified; He qualifies the called. Your weaknesses are not disqualifications in God's Kingdom. They are the very places where God shows His power and grace. True leadership starts when you stop making excuses and trust God's simple promise: *"I will be with you."*

### 4. Questions to Think About
1. What insecurity or past mistake makes you hesitate the most when given a leadership responsibility?
2. How does God's promise—*"I will be with you"*—take the pressure off your own abilities?
3. When you feel insecure, do you tend to shrink back in silence or overcompensate by trying to control everything?

### 5. Action Step
**Surrendering Your Excuse**: Write down the main excuse you use to hold back (e.g., *"I'm not good at speaking," "I'm too young/old," "I made too many mistakes"*). Draw a line through it, write Exodus 3:12 (*"I will be with you"*) next to it, and tell God: *"Lord, I am available."*

### 6. Prayer
*Father, I admit that I often look at my limitations and wonder, "Who am I?" I let fear of failure hold me back. Today, I surrender my excuses to You. Thank You that You don't ask for perfection; You ask for my willing heart. When I feel inadequate, remind me of Your promise: "I will be with you." Use me for Your glory. In Jesus' name, Amen.*

## Lesson 4: Choosing Redemption Over Revenge

### 1. Scripture Reading
**Ephesians 4:29–32 (NLT)**
> <sup>29</sup> Don’t use foul or abusive language. Let everything you say be good and helpful, so that your words will be an encouragement to those who hear them. <sup>30</sup> And do not bring sorrow to God’s Holy Spirit by the way you live. Remember, he has identified you as his own, guaranteeing that you will be saved on the day of redemption. <sup>31</sup> Get rid of all bitterness, rage, anger, harsh words, and slander, as well as all types of evil behavior. <sup>32</sup> Instead, be kind to each other, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, just as God through Christ has forgiven you.

**Colossians 3:12–13 (NLT)**
> <sup>12</sup> Since God chose you to be the holy people he loves, you must clothe yourselves with tenderhearted mercy, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience. <sup>13</sup> Make allowance for each other’s faults, and forgive anyone who offends you. Remember, the Lord forgave you, so you must forgive others.

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In the ancient Roman world, people used speech like a weapon. The culture admired leaders who were sarcastic, humiliated their rivals in public, and paid back insults immediately. Forgiving someone without getting even was seen as a sign of weakness.

The Apostle Paul taught Christians to live completely differently. He told them that our words should be building tools to encourage people, not weapons to tear them down. He also taught that leaders must forgive quickly, not because the other person earned it, but because Jesus has already forgiven us.

### 3. Using Power to Heal, Not Hurt
Every leader will eventually be let down, criticized, or misunderstood by people on their team. How you respond to that pain will decide whether your leadership brings life or toxicity to those around you.

When hurt leaders get authority, they are often tempted to get subtle revenge:
- Using sharp, sarcastic comments to put people in their place.
- Giving someone the silent treatment or freezing them out of decisions.
- Holding onto a grudge and waiting for the right moment to prove someone wrong.

Paul warns that bitterness and harsh talk hurt the Holy Spirit. Holding onto anger is like drinking poison and hoping the other person gets sick. When a leader holds a grudge, it spoils meetings, damages trust, and spreads negativity throughout the team.

The Gospel gives us a much better way: **Forgiving first, because Christ forgave us.**

Your influence was never given to you to settle personal scores or protect your ego. God gave you influence so you could show others the gentle heart of Jesus—who prayed for the very people who nailed Him to the cross: *"Father, forgive them."*

### 4. Questions to Think About
1. What are some subtle ways people in leadership "punish" others who have upset them (like sarcasm, silence, or exclusion)?
2. How does remembering how much Jesus has forgiven you make it easier to let go of an insult?
3. Is there someone on your team or in your life you have felt bitter toward lately? What is one encouraging word you could speak to build them up?

### 5. Action Step
**The Grace Challenge**: Think of one person who has annoyed or hurt you recently. Pray a simple prayer asking God to bless them and help them grow. Then, find one small, practical way to show them kindness or encouragement today.

### 6. Prayer
*Jesus, You were hurt and mocked, yet You loved and forgave. Forgive me for the times I have held onto anger or used sharp words when my pride was wounded. Fill my heart with patience, kindness, and humility. Help me speak words that build people up rather than tear them down. Make my leadership a safe place filled with Your grace. In Your name, Amen.*

## Lesson 5: Habit: Notice and Learn

### 1. Scripture Reading
**Romans 8:28, 31–32, 38–39 (NLT)**
> <sup>28</sup> And we know that God causes everything to work together for the good of those who love God and are called according to his purpose for them...
>
> <sup>31</sup> What shall we say about such wonderful things as these? If God is for us, who can ever be against us? <sup>32</sup> Since he did not spare even his own Son but gave him up for us all, won’t he also give us everything else?...
>
> <sup>38</sup> And I am convinced that nothing can ever separate us from God’s love. Neither death nor life, neither angels nor demons, neither our fears for today nor our worries about tomorrow—not even the powers of hell can separate us from God’s love. <sup>39</sup> No power in the sky above or in the earth below—indeed, nothing in all creation will ever be able to separate us from the love of God that is revealed in Christ Jesus our Lord.

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
When Paul wrote this letter, Christians living in Rome were in constant danger. They were poor, looked down upon, and faced the constant threat of arrest and execution by Roman authorities.

The Roman Emperor claimed to be the ultimate conqueror who decided everyone's fate. But Paul told these struggling believers that God is the real Judge and Protector. Even when they faced hard times, danger, or failure, nothing could ever cut them off from God’s love. They were not helpless victims; in Christ, they were *"more than conquerors."*

### 3. The Habit of Stepping Back to Learn
People often say that experience makes you wise. But that is only half true. **Experience only makes you wise if you take time to learn from it.**

If we never pause to reflect, we can make the same mistake over and over for years:
- We panic over the same problems.
- We react with anger to the same kinds of stress.
- We run ourselves into burnout the same way.

A growing leader builds the daily habit to **Notice and Learn**:
1. **Notice**: Pay attention to what happens inside you during the day. Notice when you feel anxious, when you get annoyed in a meeting, or when a kind word makes you feel encouraged.
2. **Learn**: Don’t just push those feelings down. Bring them to God and ask: *"What is this showing me about my heart? What is God trying to teach me through this situation?"*

Romans 8:28 reminds us that God is actively working in every part of our lives—not just to make things easy, but to shape us into the character of Jesus. When things go wrong, God is growing our patience. When we feel weak, God is teaching us to depend on Him.

Because nothing can ever separate you from God’s love, you don't have to fear making a mistake. You can view every challenge as a classroom where God is helping you grow.

## Lesson 6: The Cave of Testing

### 1. Scripture Reading
**1 Samuel 24:1–7, 10–12 (NLT)**
> <sup>1</sup> After Saul returned from fighting the Philistines, it was reported to him, “David is at the wilderness of En-gedi.” <sup>2</sup> So Saul chose 3,000 elite troops from all Israel and went to search for David and his men near the rocks of the wild goats. <sup>3</sup> At the place where the road passes some sheepfolds, Saul went into a cave to relieve himself. But as it happened, David and his men were hiding farther back in that very cave!
>
> <sup>4</sup> “Now’s your opportunity!” David’s men whispered to him. “Today the LORD is telling you, ‘I will certainly deliver your enemy into your power, to do with as you wish.’” So David crept forward and cut off a piece of the hem of Saul’s robe. <sup>5</sup> But then David’s conscience began bothering him because he had cut Saul’s robe. <sup>6</sup> He said to his men, “The LORD forbid that I should do this to my lord the king. I shouldn't attack the LORD’s anointed one, for the LORD himself has chosen him.” <sup>7</sup> So David restrained his men and did not let them kill Saul...
>
> <sup>10</sup> “This very day you can see with your own eyes it was the LORD who placed you in my power in the cave. Some of my men told me to kill you, but I spared you... <sup>11</sup> See what I have in my hand? It is a piece of the hem of your robe! I cut it off, but I didn't kill you. This proves that I am not trying to harm you and that I have not rebelled against you... <sup>12</sup> May the LORD judge between us. Perhaps the LORD will punish you for what you are trying to do to me, but I will never harm you.”

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In the ancient world, kings and commanders won their power through violence. If a rebel leader had the chance to kill a rival king who was hunting him down, military custom expected him to strike immediately. Everyone would have called it a great victory.

In ancient times, the hem or edge of a royal robe was a symbol of the king's royal authority. By cutting off that piece of cloth, David had proof that Saul's life had been completely in his hands. Yet David refused to kill Saul. He knew that taking a violent shortcut to the throne would go against God's ways.

### 3. Refusing the Easy Shortcut
Pressure doesn't create your character; it reveals it. When life is easy, it is simple to talk about honesty and trusting God. But when you are tired, under heavy stress, and offered an easy way out, what is really inside your heart comes to the surface.

In that dark cave, David was a hunted fugitive living with hundreds of desperate men. Suddenly, his enemy walked in alone. David's men immediately told him: *"God is handing him to you!"* The temptation was huge:
- With one swing of a sword, years of living in caves on the run would be over.
- He would become king right away.
- His men would cheer his boldness.

Yet David understood a vital truth: **You can never accomplish God's will by breaking God's principles.** 

A shortcut taken today will always destroy the spiritual trust you need tomorrow. If David had killed Saul in that cave, he would have started his reign with murder and fear. He would have taught everyone that whoever holds the biggest sword makes the rules.

As a leader, you will face "cave moments"—times where you could bend the truth, exaggerate numbers, talk behind someone's back, or take an unethical shortcut to get ahead. In those moments, remember: It is far better to stay in the cave with a clean conscience than to sit on a throne gained through compromise.

### 4. Questions to Think About
1. Have you ever felt tempted to take a shortcut (e.g., in communication, finances, or timelines) to reach a good goal? What did that situation show you about your trust in God?
2. People often use spiritual-sounding words to justify cutting corners. How can you spot the difference between God's timing and an ungodly shortcut?
3. In what area right now do you need the courage to wait for God's timing instead of forcing an outcome?

### 5. Action Step
**The Integrity Check**: Look at a project, decision, or conversation you are handling this week. Ask yourself: *"Am I trying to force this in my own strength, or am I doing it with total honesty before God?"* If you spot any manipulation, pause and give the outcome to God in prayer.

Healthy leadership requires keeping short accounts with God. When you feel that gentle check in your spirit—whether in a heated meeting, an email, or a private thought—stop immediately, confess it, and make it right.

- Public ministry is only as strong as private prayer.

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Moses was leading over a million Israelites through a harsh desert. In nomadic desert culture, the chief elder sat at the tent door to resolve every single dispute between families. Moses was trying to do this for the entire nation by himself from sunrise to sunset!

Jethro, Moses' wise father-in-law, observed this and gave him a blunt warning: *"You are going to wear yourself out—and the people too."* Jethro taught Moses to build a shared leadership system by selecting trustworthy, God-fearing leaders to handle smaller matters, leaving only the most difficult cases for Moses.

### 3. Embracing Your Limits and Empowering Others
One of the most dangerous lies in leadership is the "Superman Syndrome"—the belief that if you don't do it yourself, it won't get done right.

- He was burning himself out.
- The people were exhausted from waiting in line all day.

2. **Share the load**: Train and trust capable, godly people around you to take ownership of tasks.
3. **Protect your rest**: Rest is not a reward for finishing all your work; it is an act of trust that God is running the universe while you sleep.

When you lead within God-given limits, you protect your health, multiply leadership across your team, and build a ministry that lasts.

## Lesson 15: Habit: Record and Follow Through

### 1. Scripture Reading

In the first-century Roman city of Colossae, many believers were servants and laborers who had no legal rights or public honors. Work was often seen as degrading toil.

### 3. The Habit of Dependability
Good intentions do not equal good leadership. You can have a heart full of love and great ideas, but if you forget what you promised, drop the ball on tasks, and leave people hanging, you will lose their trust.

The church in ancient Corinth was famous for having abundant spiritual gifts—they had great speakers, prophecy, and deep knowledge. But they were also famous for having massive character problems: they sued each other in public courts, fought over favorite leaders, and treated poor members with contempt.

In Greek temples, worshippers banged large bronze gongs and cymbals to catch the attention of pagan gods—producing loud, irritating, meaningless noise. Paul told the Corinthians that having dazzling spiritual gifts without genuine, humble love makes you nothing more than a noisy, irritating gong.

### 3. Character Is the Container for Your Gifts
Here is one of the most critical lessons in Christian leadership: **Your gifts may take you to the top, but only your character will keep you there.**

There is a huge difference between spiritual *gifts* and spiritual *fruit*:
- **Gifts** are what God gives you to *do* (speaking, organizing, leading, helping).
- **Fruit** is who God shapes you to *be* (love, patience, kindness, humility, self-control).

Think of character as a glass container and your gifts as the liquid inside. If the container has cracks of pride, anger, greed, or selfishness, the liquid will leak out and make a mess. History is filled with talented, charismatic leaders whose ministries collapsed because their character was not strong enough to support their gifts.

In 1 Corinthians 13, Paul makes a staggering statement: You could have miraculous faith, world-class preaching skills, and immense knowledge, but if you treat people with harshness, arrogance, and impatience, your leadership score before God is **zero**.

God is far more interested in who you are becoming in your heart than what you produce with your hands. When the fruit of the Spirit—especially love—guides your gifts, your leadership will be safe, healthy, and life-giving to everyone you lead.

### 4. Questions to Think About
1. Why is it so easy for churches and organizations to celebrate flashy talent and gifts while overlooking character flaws?

3. How can you make sure that cultivating Christlike character remains your highest priority over simply "getting things done"?

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In the ancient world, iron was the strongest metal available. Blacksmiths had to hammer two pieces of hot iron together to shape, sharpen, and strengthen tools and swords. The process involved friction, heat, and loud blows, but it was the only way to make a dull blade sharp and effective.

In biblical wisdom, God uses honest relationships like iron sharpening iron. False friends will flatter you to your face with "many kisses," telling you only what you want to hear. But a true, godly friend has the courage to give you honest feedback to help you grow.

### 3. Becoming a Teachable Leader
Every human being has blind spots. A blind spot is a weakness, habit, or tone of voice that everyone around you sees clearly, but you cannot see yourself:
- You might think you are being "decisive," but your team feels steamrolled.
- You might think you are "laid back," but your team feels confused by lack of direction.
- You might think your jokes are funny, but they make others feel belittled.

Insecure leaders hate feedback. When someone offers a suggestion or points out a flaw, an insecure leader gets defensive, makes excuses, or attacks the person who spoke up.

Wise leaders welcome feedback as a gift. Proverbs 27:6 tells us that *"wounds from a sincere friend are better than many kisses from an enemy."* 

To grow as a leader, you must cultivate a **teachable spirit**:

3. **Ask for input proactively**: Don't wait for a crisis. Regularly ask trusted mentors and team members: *"How can I improve? What is one thing I could do differently to support you better?"*

A leader who stops learning stops leading. When you welcome godly feedback, you invite God to sharpen your character and expand your usefulness.

### 3. Turning the Leadership Pyramid Upside Down
In the world's model of leadership, the leader sits at the top of the pyramid:
- Everyone below exists to make the leader look good.
- Power is used to control people and get perks.

Servant leadership is not weak leadership. Jesus was bold, courageous, and clear. But His authority came from sacrificial love, not intimidation.

When you lead by serving, you create a culture of safety, trust, and loyalty. People don't follow you because they are forced to; they follow you because they know you genuinely care about their good.

## Lesson 23: Habit: Listen Before Responding

### 1. Scripture Reading
**James 1:19–20 (NLT)**
> <sup>19</sup> Understand this, my dear brothers and sisters: You must all be quick to listen, slow to speak, and slow to get angry. <sup>20</sup> Human anger does not produce the righteousness God desires.

**Proverbs 18:13 (NLT)**
> Spouting off before listening to the facts is both shameful and foolish.

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In the ancient world, public debate and heated arguments were popular entertainment in city squares. People cheered for debaters who interrupted opponents, spoke fast, and overwhelmed others with loud arguments.

The book of James and the book of Proverbs gave God's counter-cultural wisdom: A wise person does not rush to speak. In biblical thought, self-control over the tongue is the ultimate mark of spiritual maturity. An undisciplined tongue stirs up division, while a listening heart brings understanding and peace.

### 3. The Power of Listening
Most people do not listen to understand; they listen to reply. While someone else is talking, our minds are already formulating our rebuttal, defense, or brilliant advice.

Proverbs 18:13 gives a blunt warning: **"Spouting off before listening to the facts is both shameful and foolish."**

When leaders fail to listen:
- They make decisions based on false assumptions.
- Team members feel silenced, disrespected, and undervalued.
- Small misunderstandings blow up into major conflicts.

In the Growing Leaders journey, the fifth core habit is: **Listen Before Responding.**

1. **Be Quick to Listen**: Give people your full, undivided attention. Put down your phone, look them in the eye, and listen to both their words and their emotions.
2. **Be Slow to Speak**: Don't interrupt. Pause before you reply. Ask clarifying questions like: *"Can you tell me more about what you're seeing?"* or *"Help me understand what you mean by that."*
3. **Be Slow to Get Angry**: When someone brings a complaint or difficult feedback, breathe and stay calm. Anger clouds judgment and shuts down honest communication.

Listening is an act of love. When you listen to someone, you communicate: *"You matter to me, and your perspective is valuable."* A leader who listens well makes better decisions and earns the deep respect of their team.

### 4. Questions to Think About
1. Do you tend to interrupt people or finish their sentences when they are speaking? What drives that habit?

## Lesson 24: Peacemaking and Navigating Disagreement

### 1. Scripture Reading
**Matthew 18:15–17 (NLT)**
> <sup>15</sup> “If another believer sins against you, go privately and point out the offense. If the other person listens and confesses it, you have won that person back. <sup>16</sup> But if you are unsuccessful, take one or two others with you and go back again, so that everything you say may be confirmed by two or three witnesses. <sup>17</sup> If the person still refuses to listen, take your case to the church.”

**Romans 12:17–18 (NLT)**
> <sup>17</sup> Never pay back evil with more evil. Do things in such a way that everyone can see you are honorable. <sup>18</sup> Do all that you can to live in peace with everyone.

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In the ancient world, personal offenses were often handled through public gossip, village feuds, or violent retaliation. People talked to everyone *except* the person who actually offended them, creating widespread family and tribal division.

Jesus established a revolutionary protocol for conflict in the community: Go **privately** and directly to the person first (*"between you and him alone"*). The goal of biblical conflict resolution is never to "win the argument" or crush the other person; the goal is always **reconciliation** (*"to win back your brother"*).

### 3. The Courage of Direct, Loving Peacemaking
Conflict is inevitable in leadership. Whenever people work together toward a shared goal, differences in personality, expectations, and opinions will arise.

Conflict itself is not a sign of failure. **How you handle conflict determines whether your team grows stronger or falls apart.**

When conflict hits, people usually fall into two unhealthy extremes:
1. **The Peace-Faker (Avoidance)**: Pretends nothing is wrong, sweeps problems under the rug, and stays silent while resentment builds inside.
2. **The Peace-Breaker (Aggression)**: Attacks the person, yells, gossips to third parties, or uses power to crush disagreement.

Jesus calls us to be **Peacemakers**:
- **Go Directly and Privately**: If you have an issue with someone, talk to them directly. Never complain to coworkers or team members behind their back.
- **Assume the Best**: Approach the conversation with humility, giving the benefit of the doubt rather than assuming evil motives.
- **Focus on Reconciliation**: Your goal is not to prove you were right; your goal is to heal the relationship and find a healthy way forward together.

Peacemaking takes courage, patience, and love. But when a leader models direct, gracious conflict resolution, it creates a healthy culture of trust where problems are resolved quickly and relationships grow deeper.

### 4. Questions to Think About
1. Do you naturally tend to avoid conflict (peace-faking) or attack conflict aggressively (peace-breaking)?
2. Why is talking to a third party about an offense so damaging to a church or team environment?
3. Is there a conversation you have been avoiding because it feels uncomfortable? How can you approach it with truth and love?

### 5. Action Step
**Clear the Air**: If there is unresolved tension between you and someone else, reach out today to schedule a gentle, private conversation. Pray beforehand: *"Lord, help me speak the truth in love and seek reconciliation."*

### 6. Prayer
*Lord Jesus, You are the Prince of Peace. Give me the courage to be a true peacemaker. Deliver me from the fear of hard conversations and from the temptation to gossip. When disagreements arise, fill my heart with grace, clarity, and humility. Help me to do all that is within my power to live at peace with everyone and build unity on my team. Amen.*

## Lesson 25: Stewarding Influence and Empowering Others

### 1. Scripture Reading
**Philippians 2:3–5, 7–8 (NLT)**
> <sup>3</sup> Don’t be selfish; don’t try to impress others. Be humble, thinking of others as better than yourselves. <sup>4</sup> Don’t look out only for your own interests, but take an interest in others, too. <sup>5</sup> You must have the same attitude that Christ Jesus had... <sup>7</sup> Instead, he gave up his divine privileges; he took the humble position of a slave and was born as a human being. <sup>8</sup> When he appeared in human form, he humbled himself in obedience to God and died a criminal’s death on a cross.

**2 Timothy 2:2 (NLT)**
> You have heard me teach things that have been confirmed by many reliable witnesses. Now teach these truths to other trustworthy people who will be able to pass them on to others.

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In the Roman Empire, people hoarded power and guarded their status fiercely. Mentorship was often transactional: a patron backed a client only to increase his own political influence and social reputation.

The Apostle Paul introduced a completely different vision. In Philippians 2, he points to Jesus, who held the highest position in the universe yet voluntarily emptied Himself to serve human beings. In 2 Timothy 2:2, Paul outlines a 4-generation multiplication chain: Paul -> Timothy -> Trustworthy People -> Others. True success in Kingdom leadership is measured by how many other leaders you develop and release.

### 3. Succession and Multiplication
The ultimate test of your leadership is not what happens while you are present; it is what happens after you leave.

Insecure leaders build empires around themselves. They keep all decision-making power in their own hands, hold onto keys, and ensure that everything depends on them. When they leave, the ministry collapses.

Servant leaders build **people**:
1. **They share the spotlight**: They celebrate team members' accomplishments and give credit away generously.
2. **They invest in emerging leaders**: They spend time mentoring, training, and encouraging the next generation.
3. **They release authority**: They don't just delegate tasks (telling people what to do); they delegate authority (empowering people to make decisions and grow).

Paul told Timothy: *"Teach these truths to other trustworthy people who will be able to pass them on to others."*

Your influence is a temporary stewardship from God. Use your platform, experience, and resources not to make yourself irreplaceable, but to empower others to go further and do greater things for God's Kingdom than you ever did.

## Lesson 26: The Vine and the Branches

### 1. Scripture Reading
**John 15:4–8 (NLT)**
> <sup>4</sup> “Remain in me, and I will remain in you. For a branch cannot produce fruit if it is severed from the vine, and you cannot be fruitful unless you remain in me. <sup>5</sup> Yes, I am the vine; you are the branches. Those who remain in me, and I in them, will produce much fruit. For apart from me you can do nothing. <sup>6</sup> Anyone who does not remain in me is thrown away like a useless branch and withers... <sup>7</sup> But if you remain in me and my words remain in you, you may ask for whatever you wish, and it will be granted! <sup>8</sup> When you produce much fruit, you are my true disciples. This brings great glory to my Father.”

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In ancient Israel, vineyards covered the hillsides. Every listener understood how grapevines worked: a branch does not strain, grunt, or panic to produce grapes. The branch simply stays connected to the vine, and the life-giving sap flows naturally through it to produce sweet fruit.

If a branch is cut off from the main vine, it doesn't immediately look dead on day one. For a few days, the leaves may still look green. But because the life supply is gone, it will soon dry up, turn brown, and become useless firewood. Jesus told His disciples that our spiritual life and leadership operate on the exact same law.

### 3. Connection Before Production
Here is the ultimate law of Christian leadership: **Spiritual fruit is the natural overflow of remaining connected to Jesus, not the result of frantic human striving.**

Jesus did not say:
- *"Work harder and you will produce fruit."*
- *"Run faster and you will be effective."*

He said: **"Apart from me you can do nothing."**

Notice what happens when a leader forgets this:
- We try to lead on "autopilot" using only our natural talent, charm, and intelligence.
- We get irritable, exhausted, and dry because we are running on empty.
- We start doing things *for* God without being *with* God.

A branch cannot create its own sap. Your job as a leader is not to manufacture spiritual life; your primary job is to **abide**—to stay connected to the True Vine through daily prayer, resting in His Word, and listening to His Spirit.

When you abide in Jesus, His love, joy, patience, and wisdom flow into your life naturally. You lead from a place of spiritual overflow rather than exhaustion. The fruit you produce will be lasting, healthy, and sweet to everyone around you.

### 4. Questions to Think About
1. Are you currently leading from a place of spiritual overflow, or are you running on the fumes of exhaustion?
2. What usually cuts you off from quiet time with Jesus (e.g., busyness, phone scrolling, over-scheduling)?
3. What does "remaining in the vine" look like practically in your daily life?

### 5. Action Step
**Abiding Reset**: Set aside 15 minutes of uninterrupted quiet today. Turn off all notifications. Open your Bible, read John 15 slowly, and spend the remaining time simply resting in God's presence, thanking Him for His love.

### 6. Prayer
*Lord Jesus, You are the True Vine, and I am a branch. Forgive me for the times I have tried to produce fruit in my own strength. I confess that apart from You, I can do nothing of eternal value. Teach me how to truly abide in You. Let Your Word live in me, and let Your Spirit flow through me, that my leadership may bring lasting fruit and glory to the Father. In Your holy name, Amen.*

## Lesson 27: Mary, Martha, and the One Necessary Thing

### 1. Scripture Reading
**Luke 10:38–42 (NLT)**
> <sup>38</sup> As Jesus and the disciples continued on their way to Jerusalem, they came to a certain village where a woman named Martha welcomed him into her home. <sup>39</sup> Her sister, Mary, sat at the Lord’s feet, listening to what he taught. <sup>40</sup> But Martha was distracted by the big dinner she was preparing. She came to Jesus and said, “Lord, doesn’t it seem unfair to you that my sister just sits here while I do all the work? Tell her to come and help me.”
>
> <sup>41</sup> But the Lord said to her, “My dear Martha, you are upset and worried about many things! <sup>42</sup> There is only one thing worth being concerned about. Mary has discovered it, and it will not be taken away from her.”

**Psalm 27:4 (NLT)**
> The one thing I ask of the LORD—the thing I seek most—is to live in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, delighting in the LORD’s perfections and meditating in his Temple.

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In the ancient Near East, hospitality was a sacred cultural duty. Welcoming an esteemed Rabbi like Jesus and His twelve disciples into your home required immense physical labor: baking bread, preparing meat, fetching water, and serving multiple courses.

Martha was doing good, necessary work according to her culture. In that culture, women were strictly expected to be in the kitchen, not sitting with the men in the living room listening to a Rabbi teach. Yet Jesus defended Mary's choice. He did not criticize Martha for working; He gently corrected her for being *distracted, anxious, and bitter* while doing it.

### 3. Rescuing Your Heart from the Tyranny of the Urgent
Martha represents every well-meaning, hard-working leader:
- She loved Jesus.
- She welcomed Jesus into her home.
- She was working tirelessly to serve Him and others.

Yet look at what happened to Martha’s heart in the middle of her service:
- She became **distracted** by the tasks.
- She became **anxious and worried** about the details.
- She became **resentful** toward her sister (*"Doesn't it seem unfair to you?"*).
- She ended up **ordering Jesus around** (*"Tell her to help me!"*).

This is what happens when our *work for God* destroys our *intimacy with God*. We start out with pure motives to serve, but when we ignore our own relationship with Jesus, service turns into frantic exhaustion and bitterness.

Jesus gently whispered to Martha: **"You are worried and upset about many things, but only one thing is necessary."**

The "one thing" is sitting at Jesus' feet—listening to His voice and letting His presence fill your heart. 

Never let your service for Jesus crowd out your love for Jesus. Before you step out to manage projects, run meetings, or serve people today, take time to sit at the Master's feet. When your soul is full of His peace, you can serve others with joy rather than resentment.

### 4. Questions to Think About
1. Do you find yourself acting more like Martha (busy, stressed, and resentful) or Mary (sitting, listening, and centered)?
2. How does an overloaded schedule affect your mood, patience, and love toward the people you lead?
3. What is one non-essential activity you can cut out of your schedule this week to protect your time at Jesus' feet?

### 5. Action Step
**The "One Thing" Pause**: Before opening your emails, task lists, or social media tomorrow morning, spend the first 10 minutes sitting quietly with Jesus in prayer and Scripture. Let Him set the tone for your day.

### 6. Prayer
*Lord Jesus, I confess that I often allow the busyness of life and ministry to pull me away from Your presence. Forgive me for becoming anxious, distracted, and irritable in my work. Help me to choose the "one necessary thing." Teach me to sit at Your feet, listen to Your voice, and draw my strength from Your love. May all my service overflow from a heart of quiet worship. In Your name, Amen.*

## Lesson 28: Running the Long Race

### 1. Scripture Reading
**Hebrews 12:1–3 (NLT)**
> <sup>1</sup> Therefore, since we are surrounded by such a huge crowd of witnesses to the life of faith, let us strip off every weight that slows us down, especially the sin that so easily trips us up. And let us run with endurance the race God has set before us. <sup>2</sup> We do this by keeping our eyes on Jesus, the champion who initiates and perfects our faith. Because of the joy awaiting him, he endured the cross, disregarding its shame. Now he is seated in the place of honor beside God’s throne. <sup>3</sup> Think of all the hostility he endured from sinful people; then you won’t become weary and give up.

**1 Corinthians 9:24–26 (NLT)**
> <sup>24</sup> Don’t you realize that in a race everyone runs, but only one person gets the prize? So run to win! <sup>25</sup> All athletes are disciplined in their training. They do it to win a prize that will fade away, but we do it for an eternal prize. <sup>26</sup> So I run with purpose in every step.

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The writer of Hebrews and the Apostle Paul drew on the famous ancient Isthmian and Olympic Games of the Graeco-Roman world. Thousands of spectators filled stone stadiums to cheer on long-distance marathon runners.

Ancient runners stripped off all heavy clothing, carrying zero excess weight so they wouldn't be slowed down. They also focused their gaze straight ahead on the judge's stand (*bema*) at the finish line, ignoring the noise of the crowd. The Christian life and leadership journey is an ancient endurance race with eternal significance.

### 3. Stripping Off the Weights to Finish Strong
Leadership is not a 100-meter dash; it is a lifelong marathon. Many leaders start the race with explosive energy and great passion, but drop out halfway through because of fatigue, discouragement, or unaddressed baggage.

To run the long race with endurance, the Bible gives us three essential strategies:
1. **Strip Off the Weights**: Notice the text mentions two different things: *sins* and *weights*. Sins are clear violations of God's commands (like lust, lying, or greed). But "weights" are things that may not be sins in themselves, but they slow you down—such as over-commitment, carrying other people's expectations, unresolved past hurts, or unnecessary distractions. What weight are you carrying that is draining your stamina?
2. **Run Your Own Assigned Race**: God has set a specific course for *you*. Don't look over at the other lanes and compare your pace, calling, or platform with someone else. Run *your* race with purpose in every step.
3. **Fix Your Eyes on Jesus**: If you look at the difficulty of the road, you will get discouraged. If you look at the critics in the stands, you will get distracted. Look at Jesus! He endured the cross because He saw the joy on the other side.

When you feel weary and ready to give up, look at Jesus. He will give you the supernatural stamina to keep running faithfully until you cross the finish line.

### 4. Questions to Think About
1. What is an extra "weight" or distraction in your life right now that is slowing down your spiritual stamina?
2. Have you been tempted to compare your "race" or progress with another leader? How does Hebrews 12:1–2 bring clarity?
3. How does keeping your eyes on Jesus give you fresh strength when you feel tired or misunderstood?

### 5. Action Step
**Weight Dropping**: Identify one unnecessary obligation, toxic habit, or distraction that has been draining your energy. Take a concrete step today to remove or reduce it from your weekly routine.

### 6. Prayer
*Lord God, You have called me to run with endurance the race set before me. Forgive me for the weights and sins that easily entangle my feet. Deliver me from comparison and fatigue. Fix my eyes upon Jesus, the Author and Perfecter of my faith. Grant me spiritual stamina, joy, and purpose in every step, that I may run faithfully all the way to the end. In Jesus' mighty name, Amen.*

**Psalm 23:1, 6 (NLT)**
> <sup>1</sup> The LORD is my shepherd; I have all that I need... <sup>6</sup> Surely your goodness and unfailing love will pursue me all the days of my life, and I will live in the house of the LORD forever.

### 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Paul was writing his final letter from the cold, subterranean Mamertine Prison in Rome during Emperor Nero's severe persecution. Chained like a criminal, abandoned by former associates, and awaiting beheading, Paul knew his earthly life was ending.

Yet Paul did not write with bitterness, regret, or despair. He used the language of temple worship: his life was being poured out like a sacred drink offering (*spendomai*) on God's altar. He looked past Nero's executioner to the true, righteous Judge who was holding an eternal crown of righteousness for him in heaven.

### 3. Finishing the Race with Integrity
Leadership research shows a sobering reality: **A significant number of leaders do not finish well.** Many start out with pure hearts and great enthusiasm, but in their middle or later years, they fall to pride, moral failure, financial greed, bitterness, or complacency.

Finishing well does not mean having a problem-free life. Paul was in prison! Finishing well means:
- **You fought the good fight**: You stood firm on God's truth without compromise.
- **You finished the course**: You completed the specific assignments God gave you.
- **You kept the faith**: Your personal love and trust in Jesus remained alive and vibrant all the way to the end.

How do you guarantee that you will finish well 10, 20, or 40 years from now? You do it by guarding your heart **today**:
- Stay humble and accountable to godly friends.
- Keep your relationship with Jesus warm and personal.
- Never let success make you proud or hardship make you bitter.

Paul knew that even when human friends let him down, the Lord stood with him and gave him strength. The same Shepherd who guided you on Day 1 of your journey will guide you all the way home into His heavenly Kingdom.

### 6. Prayer

When I am weak, stand by me and give me strength. Keep my eyes fixed on the eternal prize of knowing You. May my entire life be a joyful offering of worship to Jesus Christ. In His name, Amen.*
