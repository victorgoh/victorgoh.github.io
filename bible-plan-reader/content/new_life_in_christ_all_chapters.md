# Chapter 1: How Can I Be Sure I Am Saved?

**Source:** *New Life In Christ: A Discipleship Guide for New Believers* (Pages 1–4)

---

## A. Because the Bible Says So

A person becomes a Christian by **trusting in Jesus Christ alone** and what He has done. This trust is our faith.

We can be sure of our salvation because of the assurance from **1 John 5:13**:
> *"These things I have written to you who believe in the name of the Son of God, that you may know that you have eternal life, and that you may continue to believe in the name of the Son of God."*

It is possible to feel unsure of ourselves and the commitment we have made. After receiving the gift of eternal life, new believers may sometimes:
- Feel confused
- Have doubts about God
- Feel nothing
- Wonder if God really heard our prayer
- Be afraid that parents, family, or colleagues might oppose us

Being unsure is an experience common to many new Christians. However, irrespective of our doubts, we can be confident of our salvation by looking at what God has done.

**Additional Scripture References:**
- **John 1:12** — Believers are given the right to become children of God.
- **John 3:36** — Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life.
- **1 John 1:9** — Confession brings forgiveness and cleansing.
- **1 John 4:14, 15** — Confessing Jesus as the Son of God means God abides in us.
- **1 John 5:4, 5** — Our faith overcomes the world.
- **1 John 5:12** — He who has the Son has life.
- **Romans 10:9, 10** — Confessing with the mouth and believing in the heart brings salvation.
- **Hebrews 6:15, 16** — God's promise and oath provide unwavering assurance.

---

## B. Because of What Jesus Has Done

The gospel (good news) of Jesus Christ is based on five indisputable facts:

1. **God loves us** (**Romans 5:8**).
2. **God sent His only Son into the world to save mankind** (**John 3:16**).
3. **This salvation was achieved when the Lord Jesus died on the cross** to pay the penalty for our sins and purchase a place in heaven for us.
   > *"For He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might become the righteousness of God in Him."* — **2 Corinthians 5:21**
4. **This was acceptable to God because God raised Jesus from the dead** after the third day (**1 Peter 1:21**).
5. **Jesus is now in heaven and will soon return to earth** for those who have trusted in Him and to establish His kingdom (**1 Thessalonians 4:14–17**).
   > *"So they said, 'Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ and you will be saved, you and your household.'"* — **Acts 16:31**

As Acts 16:31 states, when a person believes in the Lord Jesus, he is saved. **Believing in Jesus Christ is the only requirement for being saved—nothing else.**

---

## C. Because Our Lives Are Changed

Some people change instantly after accepting Jesus Christ into their lives, while others change progressively over a period of time.

Turn to **Luke 19:1–10** (Zacchaeus) and **1 Timothy 1:12–13** (Paul) to see examples of the transforming change God brings about in individuals. Whatever our background, God desires to change us and make us more like Him. 

We should actively cooperate with God as He highlights areas in our lives that require transformation.

---

## D. Because of the Facts

Sometimes we 'feel' the presence of God; other times we do not. Sometimes we 'feel' more 'spiritual'; other times we do not. Fluctuating emotions are a natural fact of human life.

However, our emotions are **NOT a trustworthy barometer of our spirituality**. Jesus promised in **Matthew 28:20** that He is with us always—regardless of whether we 'feel' His presence or not.

### The Train Illustration (Fact, Faith, Feelings)

Consider how a steam train operates:

```
+---------------+      +---------------+      +---------------+
|     ENGINE    | ===> |      COAL     | ===> |   CARRIAGE    |
|    (FACT)     |      |    (FAITH)    |      |  (FEELINGS)   |
+---------------+      +---------------+      +---------------+
```

- **Engine = FACT:** The truth revealed in God's Word.
- **Coal = FAITH:** Believing the facts, which feeds the engine and makes the truth operative in our lives.
- **Carriage = FEELINGS:** Good to have, but in no way essential to the operation of the facts of God's Word. Feelings are dependent on Fact and Faith—**never the other way around.**

We may 'feel' more assured of going to heaven on some days than on others. However, as long as our faith is anchored in Jesus Christ, we can be fully assured of our salvation regardless of how we feel.

---

## E. What If I Still Sin?

As Christians, being assured of salvation does not mean we are instantly perfect. We will still commit sins; however, the key difference now is that we are much more conscious of sin when it occurs.

As we grow and mature in our Christian faith, we become increasingly sensitive to sin and develop a heart's desire to sin less, even though we may stumble occasionally.

### What to Do When We Sin:
1. **Confess our sins:** **1 John 1:9** assures us that if we confess our sins, God is faithful and just to forgive us and cleanse us (**John 8:11**).
2. **Resolve to sin no more:** After confessing, we must turn away from sin and resolve not to repeat it.

Read **Philippians 1:6**: *Jesus has begun a good work in us, and He will complete it.* Once we have accepted Jesus as our Lord and Savior, He begins the ongoing work of perfecting us until it is brought to completion. 

Our primary responsibility is to continue walking closely with Him and developing our personal relationship with Him daily.

# Chapter 2: The Word of God: Bible

**Source:** *New Life In Christ: A Discipleship Guide for New Believers* (Pages 5–10)

---

## A. Introduction & Overview

The Bible is not just one single book, but rather a library of **66 books bound together**. These books were written by approximately **44 writers** under the direction of the Holy Spirit over a period exceeding **1,000 years**.

Turn to the index in your Bible to see all 66 books listed, which are divided into two main sections:

### 1. Old Testament
Pertains to the period before Jesus Christ's birth (39 Books):
- **The Law (5 Books):** Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy
- **History (12 Books):** Joshua, Judges, Ruth, 1 & 2 Samuel, 1 & 2 Kings, 1 & 2 Chronicles, Ezra, Nehemiah, Esther
- **Poetry (5 Books):** Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Solomon
- **Major Prophets (5 Books):** Isaiah, Jeremiah, Lamentations, Ezekiel, Daniel
- **Minor Prophets (12 Books):** Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi

> *Note: The distinction between Major and Minor Prophets is based purely on the length of their messages. A Minor Prophet's message from God was shorter than that of a Major Prophet, but not any less important.*

### 2. New Testament
Pertains to the time during and after Jesus Christ's time on earth (27 Books):
- **The Gospels (4 Books):** Matthew, Mark, Luke, John
- **History (1 Book):** Acts
- **Pauline Epistles (14 Books):** Romans, 1 & 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, 1 & 2 Thessalonians, 1 & 2 Timothy, Titus, Philemon, Hebrews
- **General Epistles (7 Books):** James, 1 & 2 Peter, 1, 2 & 3 John, Jude
- **Prophecy (1 Book):** Revelation

---

## B. What Are the Uses of the Bible?

The Bible is also referred to as the **"Scriptures"**, **"Word of God"**, and **"Law"**.

### Inspired by God
How much of the Bible is inspired by God? **ALL of it.**
> *(This is important because some people claim that not all parts of the Bible were inspired by God).*

> *"All Scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness, that the man of God may be complete, thoroughly equipped for every good work."* — **2 Timothy 3:16–17**

According to **2 Timothy 3:16–17**, the Bible is profitable for four main purposes:
1. **Doctrine:** Teachings or principles of truth.
2. **Reproof:** Revealing one's errors.
3. **Correction:** Making right the errors revealed.
4. **Instruction in Righteousness:** Instruction on how to walk right before God.

*Result:* The Word of God will **completely equip** a person for every good work.

---

### Four Key Uses and Metaphors of God's Word

1. **A Lamp and Light for Guidance**
   > *"Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path."* — **Psalms 119:105**
   - God's Word gives us divine guidance when we do not know how to handle difficult situations in life.

2. **Spiritual Food Necessary for Life**
   > *"But He answered and said, 'It is written, "Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God."'"* — **Matthew 4:4**
   - Just as physical food is needed for physical life, God's Word is essential for a Christian's spiritual life.

3. **A Living and Discerning Sword**
   > *"For the word of God is living and powerful, and sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing even to the division of soul and spirit, and of joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart."* — **Hebrews 4:12**
   - The Word of God cuts deep to discern the true thoughts and intentions of the human heart (**Ephesians 6:17**).

4. **The Sword of the Spirit Against Temptation**
   - The Bible is described as the **sword of the Spirit** (**Ephesians 6:17**).
   - When Satan attacks or tempts us, we can stand firmly on the promises contained in God's Word. Satan can never refute God's truth.
   - When Satan tempted Jesus in the wilderness, Jesus refuted him every time by declaring, *"It is written..."*

---

## C. What Should We Do with the Word of God?

1. **Meditate on it:**
   - *"This Book of the Law shall not depart from your mouth, but you shall meditate in it day and night..."* (**Joshua 1:8**).
   - Do not turn from it to the left or right, and do not twist scripture to fulfill selfish desires.
   - *Promise:* As we meditate on and obey God's Word, God promises to grant us success and prosperity.

2. **Practice it in daily life:**
   - *"But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves."* (**James 1:22**).
   - Whatever instructions and advice are contained in the Bible should be put into practice.

3. **Study it diligently:**
   - *"Be diligent to present yourself approved to God, a worker who does not need to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth."* — **2 Timothy 2:15**
   - Be diligent in studying the Word to know how to handle and interpret it correctly.

4. **Hold fast to it:**
   - *"Hold fast the pattern of sound words which you have heard from me, in faith and love which are in Christ Jesus."* (**2 Timothy 1:13**).
   - Continually pay attention to God's Word and never neglect it.

**Additional Scripture References for Study:**
- **Matthew 7:24–27** (Building one's life on the rock of God's Word)
- **Joshua 1:8** (Meditating for success)
- **Ecclesiastes 12:13** (Fearing God and keeping His commandments)
- **Psalms 119:9, 11, 105** (Cleansing one's way and hiding God's Word in the heart)
- **1 Peter 2:2** (Desiring the pure milk of the Word to grow)
- **Romans 10:17** (Faith comes by hearing the Word of God)

## D. How to Read the Bible & Look Up References

### 1. Introduction
The Bible consists of 66 books written by 44 authors over 1,000 years. To locate any passage, turn to the index at the front of your Bible.

### 2. How Bible References Are Written
Bible references are abbreviated using standard formats:
- **"2 Cor 5:7"** means **2 Corinthians Chapter 5, Verse 7**.
- **"John 14:6"** means **Gospel of John Chapter 14, Verse 6**.
- Book names are normally abbreviated using the first 3 or 4 letters (e.g., *Genesis* = **Gen**, *Isaiah* = **Isa**).

### 3. How to Locate a Verse on a Bible Page
When looking at a page in the Bible:
- **Chapter Number:** Denoted by the **large bold number** at the beginning of a section/paragraph.
- **Verse Number:** Denoted by the **small number** at the beginning of a sentence.

**Example Lookup — John 10:10:**
1. Find the book of **John**.
2. Look for the large bold chapter number **10**.
3. Locate small verse number **10** within that chapter.

> *"The thief does not come except to steal, and to kill, and to destroy. I have come that they may have life, and that they may have it more abundantly."* — **John 10:10**

# Chapter 3: Prayer

**Source:** *New Life In Christ: A Discipleship Guide for New Believers* (Pages 11–14)

---

## A. What Is Prayer?

**Prayer is simply talking to God.** 

Prayer is a sacred privilege—the same privilege a child enjoys with his or her loving father. Although God is the Creator and Ruler of all things, we are invited to approach Him directly with childlike trust and confidence. What an incredible privilege!

---

## B. Why Should Believers Pray?

1. **We are encouraged to pray** (**1 Timothy 2:8**): God explicitly desires that believers everywhere pray with holy hands and pure hearts.
2. **Focuses our attention on God**: When we pray, our minds and hearts center on Him. It provides the essential opportunity to cultivate and deepen our personal relationship with God.
3. **Builds us up spiritually** (**Jude 1:20**): Praying in the Holy Spirit strengthens our inner spiritual life and builds us up in our most holy faith.
4. **Follows Jesus' example** (**Mark 1:35**): Jesus Himself regularly prioritized spending time in solitude and prayer with the Father.

---

## C. Elements of Prayer (The A.C.T.S. Model)

A balanced, healthy prayer life includes four key elements (easily remembered using the acronym **A.C.T.S.**):

1. **Adoration** — Worshiping God for who He is—His character, holiness, majesty, and love.
2. **Confession** — Confessing our sins before God, acknowledging where we have fallen short, and receiving His forgiveness.
3. **Thanksgiving** — Expressing gratitude and giving thanks to God for all that He has done, provided, and answered.
4. **Supplication** — Bringing our personal needs, requests, and intercessions for others before God.

---

## D. A Model Prayer (The Lord's Prayer)

Jesus provided a foundational model for prayer in **Matthew 6:9–13**. This pattern guides us on how to structure our communication with God:

- **"Our Father who is in Heaven, hallowed be Your name"**  
  *Focus:* Centering our minds on who our Heavenly Father is and honoring His holy name.
- **"Your kingdom come, Your will be done on earth as it is in heaven"**  
  *Submission:* Seeking God's sovereign will and asking for His guidance, especially when making important life decisions.
- **"Give us today our daily bread"**  
  *Provision:* Bringing our daily physical, material, and spiritual needs before God. Nothing in our lives is too small or unimportant for Him.
- **"Forgive us our sins (debts) as we forgive those who sin against us (debtors)"**  
  *Pardon & Reconciliation:* Asking God to forgive our daily transgressions, while actively seeking His help to extend forgiveness to those who have wronged us.
- **"Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil"**  
  *Protection:* Asking God to shield us from sinful traps, spiritual hazards, and physical harm.
- **"For Yours is the kingdom, the power and the glory forever. Amen."**  
  *Praise:* Concluding our prayer with heartfelt praise for God's eternal power, majesty, and authority.

---

## E. Attitudes in Prayer

When approaching God in prayer, we must cultivate specific inner attitudes as taught in Scripture:

1. **Faith** (**Hebrews 11:6**):  
   > *"But without faith it is impossible to please Him, for he who comes to God must believe that He is, and that He is a rewarder of those who diligently seek Him."*  
   *Application:* We must pray with firm confidence, believing that God hears and will answer our prayers.

2. **A Grateful Heart** (**Philippians 4:6**):  
   > *"Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God."*  
   *Application:* We present our requests with a thankful heart, praising Him in advance for His faithful answers.

3. **Alignment with God's Will** (**1 John 5:14–15**):  
   > *"Now this is the confidence that we have in Him, that if we ask anything according to His will, He hears us."*  
   *Application:* We should pray for things knowing that God approves of them according to His revealed Word.

4. **Sincerity** (**Matthew 6:5–8**):  
   *Application:* We must pray with genuine honesty. God rejects hypocritical displays or empty repetitions intended to impress others.

---

## F. Hindrances to Prayer

Scripture warns that certain attitudes or behaviors can hinder or obstruct our prayers:

1. **Wrong Motives** (**James 4:1–3**): Asking out of selfish ambition, pride, or worldly pleasures.
2. **Pride** (**James 4:6**): God opposes and resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble.
3. **Unconfessed Sin** (**Isaiah 59:2**):  
   > *"But your iniquities have separated you from your God; and your sins have hidden His face from you, so that He will not hear."*  
   *Application:* Living right before God and confessing known sin is essential for open communion and answered prayer.
4. **Unforgiveness** (**Matthew 6:14–15**; **Psalm 66:18**): Holding bitterness, grudges, or resentment against others blocks God's forgiveness and hinders our prayers.
5. **Wrong Timing**: God answers prayers according to His perfect timing and wisdom, not our demands. His answer may be *"Yes"*, *"No"*, or *"Wait"*.

---

## G. Conclusion & Practical Exercise

> *"Prayer works; prayer is work; prayer leads to work."*

The great fault of many Christians is not that they never pray, but that they do not **continue** in prayer—they fail to persevere. 

**Practical Action Step:**  
Keep a **Prayer Notebook / Journal** to record your specific prayer requests alongside the date and manner in which God answers them. Reviewing your prayer journal regularly is a deeply encouraging spiritual exercise as you count the Lord's blessings and witness His faithfulness over time!

# Chapter 4: Quiet Time

**Source:** *New Life In Christ: A Discipleship Guide for New Believers* (Pages 15–22)

---

## A. What Is Quiet Time?

**Quiet Time** is a daily time of personal fellowship with God, spent in His Word and in prayer. 

It is a time we deliberately set aside to meet with God. The objective is to:
- Grow in our personal relationship with God.
- See Him more clearly, know Him more dearly, and become more like Him daily.
- Hear from God and allow Him to teach us His ways.

---

## B. Why Do I Need to Do Quiet Time?

1. **Created for Fellowship:** Man was created in God's own image for the purpose of fellowship. We are the only creatures in all creation with the capacity to fellowship with Him.
2. **Developing Our Relationship:** God wants us to develop our personal relationship with Him, which can only happen by spending regular time with Him.
3. **Spiritual Nourishment:** We cannot be healthy, growing Christians without daily fellowship with God. Just as our physical body requires daily food to remain healthy, our spiritual life requires daily Quiet Time.

> **Psalms 119:97**  
> *"Oh, how I love Your law! It is my meditation all the day."*  
> *(Lesson: We are to love God's Word and meditate on it continuously).*

---

## C. How Do I Do Quiet Time? (The 4 R's)

Everyone has their own method, but a fruitful Quiet Time generally follows these **4 R's**:

### 1. Reading
Read a section of the Bible slowly, repeatedly, and aloud. Reading the passage a few times helps capture the true meaning of what is being said.

### 2. Reflect & Remember (Meditation)
Meditate on what you have read and note the verses that speak to you personally. Ask yourself these questions:
- Is there an **Example** for me to follow?
- Is there a **Command** for me to obey?
- Is there an **Error** for me to avoid?
- Is there a **Sin** for me to forsake?
- Is there a **Promise** for me to claim?
- Are there **New thoughts** about God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit?  
*(Note: Not all questions will apply every time).*

### 3. Record
Write down in a journal what God has revealed to you during your reading and meditation.

### 4. Request (A Time of Prayer)
Speak to God through prayer. Bring your needs before Him, ask for guidance, and tell Him about your problems. *(Refer to Chapter 3 for more details on prayer).*

---

## D. Practical Aspects & Building the Habit

### 1. Practical Guidelines
- **Make it a Priority:** Just as people set priorities for social or recreational routines (e.g., sports nights, shopping), Christians must make Quiet Time a top daily priority.
- **Select a Specific Time:** The best time is when you are at your best (mornings for early birds, evenings for night owls). Jesus rose early to spend time with God (**Mark 1:35**). Choose a fixed time to help it become a daily routine.
- **Choose a Special Place:** Find a quiet, solitary place without interruptions (**Mark 1:35**).
- **Gather Materials:**
  - A reliable Bible translation (e.g., NKJV, NIV, or NLT).
  - A notebook/journal to record insights and maintain a prayer list.

### 2. Making Quiet Time a Habit
Research shows that it takes approximately **3 weeks (21 days) of consistent practice** for an activity to become a habit. 
- Ask God for perseverance as you establish this practice.
- While forming a new habit requires effort, daily Quiet Time yields tremendous spiritual blessings.

---

## E. How to Do S.O.A.P. Devotions & Journaling

> *“Blessed are they whose ways are blameless, who walk according to the law of the LORD. Blessed are they who keep his statutes and seek him with all their heart.”* — **Psalm 119:1–2**

### 1. Why Journal Your Devotions?
Daily devotions shape our thinking and transform our mind so that we respond to life's situations as Jesus did. Journaling helps us:
- **Record & Process:** Write down and process what God speaks to us.
- **Retain Blessings:** Keep a record of spiritual "gems" so key lessons are not forgotten over time.
- **Share & Encourage:** Share insights with family or friends to foster deeper discussion and mutual encouragement.

### 2. The S.O.A.P. Method Defined
- **S — Scripture:** Open your Bible to your reading plan. Read carefully, identify 1–2 key verses that speak to you, and write them in your journal.
- **O — Observation:** Ask the Holy Spirit to teach you and reveal Jesus. Paraphrase what God is saying in your own words.
- **A — Application:** Personalize the text. Ask: *"How does this apply to my life right now?"* (Instruction, encouragement, promise, or correction).
- **P — Prayer:** Write out a prayer asking God to help you apply the scripture. Listen to what God says, as prayer is a two-way conversation.

> **Encouragement for Beginners:** If journaling feels overwhelming, start simple! Show up for your daily appointment—God is always waiting. Even if you only understand 10% of a passage, journal on what you do understand!

### 3. Getting Started Checklist
- **Get a Journal:** Use a notebook or devotional journal to write daily entries.
- **Select a Reading Plan:** Begin with a structured plan (e.g., starting with the Gospel of Matthew).
- **Set Your Schedule:** Choose the best 25 minutes of your day—whether morning, lunchtime, or evening. Jesus deserves your best!

# Chapter 5: Overcoming Temptation

**Source:** *New Life In Christ: A Discipleship Guide for New Believers* (Pages 23–26)

---

## A. God's Standard & What Is Temptation?

### 1. God's Way: The Call to Holiness
God desires all His children to live holy, blameless lives:

> *" ...because it is written, 'be holy for I am holy.'"* — **1 Peter 1:16**

- **Sin Defined:** Any act, thought, or word that falls short of God's perfect standards.
- **Human Reality:** As Christians, we are not yet perfect and may occasionally yield to temptation. Learning to overcome temptation so that we may live holy lives is a challenge every believer faces.

### 2. What Is Temptation?
The word *temptation* means **"to test, to try, to prove."** Temptation occurs when a person's will is tested to see whether he or she will commit sin.

> *"For all that is in the world—the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life—is not of the Father but is of the world."* — **1 John 2:16**

Temptation manifests in three primary areas:
1. **Lust of the Flesh:** Craving physical or sensual gratification (e.g., illicit sex, drugs, drunkenness).
2. **Lust of the Eyes:** Coveting material possessions one sees (e.g., expensive cars, houses, luxury items).
3. **Pride of Life:** Pursuit of status or self-glorification (e.g., career status, wealth, fame).

*(Note: Lust is defined as any excessive, selfish desire.)*

> **Key Truth:** Temptation itself is **not** a sin. It is *yielding* to temptation that constitutes sin. Whenever a Christian is tempted, he or she has the power to choose whether to sin or not to sin.

---

## B. What Is the Danger of Continually Yielding to Temptation?

Yielding repeatedly to sinful desires sets off a dangerous spiritual chain reaction:

> *"But each one is tempted when he is drawn away by his own desires and enticed. Then, when desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin; and sin, when it is full-grown, brings forth death."* — **James 1:14–15**

A person is tempted by his own internal desires. If he continually yields to them, the ultimate outcome is physical and spiritual death.

### What to Do When We Stumble
When we stumble into sin, we must immediately take two action steps:
1. **Confess Our Sins:** As promised in **1 John 1:9**, when we confess, God is faithful to forgive us and cleanse us from all unrighteousness.
2. **Repent:** (**Luke 3:8a**) Repentance is more than just feeling sorry—it is a firm, decisive resolve **not to commit that sin again**.

### God's Promise of Escape
When facing temptation, we have God's faithful guarantee of protection:

> *"No temptation has overtaken you except such as is common to man; but God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but with the temptation will also make the way of escape, that you may be able to bear it."* — **1 Corinthians 10:13**

God will never allow us to be tempted beyond our ability to resist; He always provides a way of escape. So the next time you are tempted, remember: **God knows exactly how much you can resist!**

---

## C. How Can I Overcome Temptation? (Seven Practical Steps)

1. **Study, Memorize, Meditate On, and Obey God's Word:**
   Hide Scripture in your heart as a shield against sin.
   > *"Your word I have hidden in my heart, that I might not sin against You."* — **Psalms 119:11**

2. **Flee From Temptation:**
   Run away from the source of temptation; do not linger in its presence to test your willpower.
   > *"Flee youthful lusts; but pursue righteousness, faith, love, peace with those who call on the Lord out of a pure heart."* — **2 Timothy 2:22**
   
   * *Example:* If you had a past struggle with alcohol, do not linger near pubs to test your strength. Consider **Joseph in Genesis 39**: when Potiphar's wife attempted to seduce him, he did not hesitate or argue—he immediately ran away.

3. **Guard Your Thought Life:**
   Sin almost always begins in the mind.
   > *"For as he thinks in his heart, so is he..."* — **Proverbs 23:7**
   
   While we cannot always prevent sinful thoughts from entering our minds, we control what we do with them. When a sinful thought occurs, reject it immediately rather than dwelling on it.

4. **Watch and Pray:**
   Stay spiritually alert through regular prayer.
   > *"Watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation. The spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak."* — **Matthew 26:41**
   
   Spending time in God's presence makes us more conscious of His holiness and helps us guard against areas where we are vulnerable to falling.

5. **Get Busy in the Lord's Service:**
   An idle mind is the devil's playground. King David fell into adultery with Bathsheba because he stayed home in idle leisure instead of leading his army on the battlefield (**2 Samuel 11:1–4**). When we are actively engaged in serving God, we have no time or room for sinful pursuits.

6. **Seek the Fellowship of Other Believers:** (**2 Timothy 2:22**)
   Surround yourself with righteous friends. As you spend time with mature Christians, their exemplary conduct, faith, and lifestyle will rub off on you.

7. **Secure the Help of a Mature Christian or Cell Leader:**
   If you struggle to overcome a persistent temptation, do not fight alone—seek spiritual counsel, guidance, and prayer from mature believers.
   > *"Brethren, if a man is overtaken in any trespass, you who are spiritual restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness, considering yourself, lest you also be tempted."* — **Galatians 6:1**

## Conclusion & Promise of Reward

We can rejoice knowing that enduring temptation brings eternal honor:

> *"Blessed is the man who endures temptation; for when he has been approved, he will receive the crown of life which the Lord has promised to those who love Him."* — **James 1:12**

As we remain faithful and endure temptation, we have the glorious promise of God's reward awaiting us at the end!

## A. What Is Fellowship?

The word "fellowship" comes from the Greek word ***koinonia***, which means **participation, sharing, or partnership**. In relation to God's people, it describes a partnership, communion, or relationship that believers enter into by virtue of their common experience of salvation and mutual participation in the Body of Christ.

### The Charcoal Illustration
An individual Christian is like a piece of burning charcoal:
- **In isolation:** A single charcoal ember removed from the fire quickly cools down and stops burning altogether.
- **In community:** Placed among other burning charcoals, it stays vibrant, hot, and glowing.

> *"It has been said that no man is an island. The same is true for Christians. Fellowship is essential for spiritual growth and vitality."*

### Our Mutual Responsibility
It is the active responsibility of all Christians in fellowship to:
- **Encourage** one another
- **Guide** one another
- **Comfort** one another
- **Love** one another
- **Edify (build up)** one another

---

## B. What Are the Two Aspects of Christian Fellowship?

Christian fellowship operates in two essential directions:

```
          [ GOD ]
             ^
             |  (1) Vertical Fellowship
             v
    [ BELIEVER ] <=====> [ FELLOW BELIEVERS ]
                  (2) Horizontal Fellowship
```

1. **Vertical Fellowship (God-ward):**
   - **Foundation:** Fellowship with God is the foundation of all true Christian fellowship.
   - **Access:** Made possible when we accept Jesus Christ as our Lord and Savior.

2. **Horizontal Fellowship (Man-ward):**
   - **Community:** Once vertical fellowship with God is established, we can enjoy meaningful horizontal fellowship with fellow believers in the Body of Christ.

---

## C. Expressing Christian Fellowship in Daily Life

All Christians need two complementary church gatherings:
- **Sunday Worship Service:** In the larger local church gathering, to grow spiritually by hearing the Word of God.
- **Cell / Home Fellowship Meetings:** In smaller group gatherings, to build personal relationships and relate with other believers.

To make Christian fellowship meaningful, we should practice these **7 key expressions**:

1. **Pray for One Another**
   > *"Confess your trespasses to one another, and pray for one another, that you may be healed. The effective, fervent prayer of a righteous man avails much."* — **James 5:16**
   - Pray unselfishly for others, not only for ourselves.
   - **Exhort & Encourage:** Consistently encourage fellow believers, especially through difficult times (**Hebrews 3:13; 10:25**).

   - Do not hold grudges or harbor bitterness if someone wrongs you.

3. **Receive One Another as Friends** (**Romans 15:7**)
   - Open your heart and warmly receive others into your circle of friendship.

4. **Serve One Another** (**Galatians 5:13b**)
   - Adopt a servant's heart toward fellow brothers and sisters in Christ.

5. **Be Considerate of One Another** (**Ephesians 4:32**)
   - Show genuine care and consideration for others' feelings, needs, and struggles.

6. **Help the Needy Financially** (**1 John 3:16–18**)
   - Share material blessings and help meet tangible financial needs within the body.

7. **Get to Know and Appreciate Each Other**
   - Invest time in building deep, uplifting relationships.

   > *"Do not be deceived: 'Evil company corrupts good habits.'"* — **1 Corinthians 15:33**

# Chapter 7: Stewardship and Giving

**Source:** *New Life In Christ: A Discipleship Guide for New Believers* (Pages 31–34)

---

## A. What Is a Steward and What Is Stewardship?

A **steward** is someone who has been **entrusted with and manages** the properties and/or affairs belonging to another person. In the New Testament, a steward is one entrusted to manage and guard the household of another.

**Stewardship** is the job, responsibility, or duty of managing and guarding another person's property or affairs. 

> **Illustration:** If a man allows you to use his car while he is overseas, he remains the owner; you are merely the steward responsible for taking care of the car.

### A Believer's Perspective on Ownership vs. Stewardship
According to **Psalms 24:1**, God is the ultimate Owner and Possessor of all things in heaven and on earth (*"The earth is the Lord's, and all its fullness, the world and those who dwell therein"*).

As believers, our perspective on everything we possess should be:
> *"I am a steward of what God has placed in my hands. Lord, what would You have me do with Your properties and the affairs You have entrusted to me?"*

| Ownership (God) | Stewardship (Man) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Owner** | **Steward** |
| **Giver** | **Receiver** |
| **Possessor** | **Manager** |
| **Rewarder** | **Rewarded** |

---

## B. Everyone Is Accountable to God

Since we are stewards rather than owners, it is vital that we manage what God has entrusted to us according to the Owner's wishes. Scripture reveals three primary areas of stewardship:

1. **Lives (Acts 17:25):** Our lives are not our own; they belong to God. We must ask God what He would have us do with our lives.
2. **Time (Psalms 90:12):** Time is a precious resource that should not be wasted. God wants us to make full and wise use of our time.
3. **Talents & Possessions (Matthew 25:14–30):** Each of us has God-given talents and resources. We are expected to develop them and use them faithfully for His glory.

### Character Qualities God Requires of Stewards
God expects specific character qualities in those managing His affairs:

- **A. Faithful (Luke 16:10):** *"He who is faithful in what is least is faithful also in much; and he who is unjust in what is least is unjust also in much."* We must handle even the smallest matters according to God's wishes.
- **B. Accountable (Romans 14:12):** Each of us will be required to give a personal account of our stewardship to God.
- **C. Servant's Heart (Galatians 5:13b):** We are called to serve God and others with all that He has placed in our care.

> *Transitional Principle:* Managing our **finances** is one major area that requires good stewardship.

---

## C. Where Does Stewardship of Our Finances Begin?

The stewardship of our finances begins with the practice of giving our **Tithes and Offerings** (**Malachi 3:7, 10**).

- **What is a Tithe?** **Leviticus 27:30–33** defines a tithe as **one-tenth (10%)** of our earnings or allowance returned to God as holy.
- **What is an Offering?** An offering is any voluntary gift given to God above and beyond the tithe.

### Six Biblical Principles of Tithing (Malachi 3:8–11)

1. **Failure to tithe is considered robbing God (v. 8):** Just as withholding income tax is robbing the government of what is rightfully theirs, withholding tithes and offerings is robbing God of what belongs to Him.
2. **Tithes are brought to the Storehouse (v. 10a):** In Old Testament times, tithes supported the priests and temple operations. Today, tithes support church workers and local church ministries. Each Sunday worship service provides an opportunity to give our tithes and offerings.
3. **God invites us to test Him in tithing (v. 10b):** While we are strictly forbidden from testing God in other situations, tithing is the sole exception where God invites us to test His faithfulness to bless us.
4. **Abundant Spiritual & Material Blessings (v. 10b):** As we give, God promises to open the windows of heaven and pour out blessings so great that there will not be room enough to receive them.
5. **Protection from the Devourer (v. 11a):** God promises to *"rebuke the devourer for our sakes,"* preventing Satan from destroying, wasting, or robbing our resources.
6. **Fruitfulness & Success (v. 11b):** God causes our labor and efforts to bear fruit, granting success as we remain faithful in giving.

> **Note on God's Blessings:** We must not equate God's blessings solely with financial or material gain. God's blessings also include good health for our families, inner peace, joy, divine protection, and favor with others.

---

## D. Principles of Generous Giving (A Last Word on Giving)

In **2 Corinthians 9:6–8**, the Apostle Paul outlines key principles for financial giving:

1. **Law of Sowing and Reaping (v. 6):** *"He who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and he who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully."* If we desire God to bless us generously, we must first give generously to Him.
2. **Give Cheerfully (v. 7):** *"God loves a cheerful giver."* We should give willingly from the heart, not grudgingly or under compulsion. (Just as we would be reluctant to accept a gift from a friend who complained about how hard it was to give, God desires joyful givers).
3. **God Ensures Sufficiency (v. 8):** As we give to God, He promises to make all grace abound toward us so that we always have all sufficiency in all things.

# Chapter 8: Water Baptism and Holy Communion

**Source:** *New Life In Christ: A Discipleship Guide for New Believers* (Pages 35–38)

## A. Two Commandment Ordinances

The only ongoing ordinances that Jesus Christ commands His disciples to observe are **Water Baptism** and **Holy Communion**. 

---

## B. Water Baptism

### 1. What Is Water Baptism?
The word *baptized* literally means **'to immerse or to submerge'**. To water-baptize someone means to completely submerge that person in water.

> **The Marriage Analogy:**
> When two people get married, they hold a wedding ceremony as a public declaration of their love and intention to live as husband and wife. Similarly, when a Christian gets water-baptized, it serves as a **public declaration** of their faith in Jesus Christ and their commitment to be His disciple.

---

### 2. Why Should a Christian Get Water-Baptized?

#### a) Public Declaration of Faith
Confessing Jesus publicly before others is vital for every believer:

> *"Therefore whoever confesses Me before men, him I will also confess before My Father who is in heaven. But whoever denies Me before men, him I will also deny before My Father who is in heaven."* — **Matthew 10:32–33**

Jesus assures us that when we confess Him openly before others, He will acknowledge and confess us before God in heaven.

#### b) Direct Command of Christ
Baptism is an integral component of the Great Commission given to all disciples:

> *"Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit."* — **Matthew 28:19**

---

### 3. Spiritual Significance of Water Baptism

> *"Or do you not know that as many of us as were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death? Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death, that just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life. For if we have been united together in the likeness of His death, certainly we also shall be in the likeness of His resurrection."* — **Romans 6:3–5**

Based on **Romans 6:3–5**, water baptism symbolizes our spiritual identification with three key events:

1. **Jesus Christ's Death:** Going into the water acknowledges that our old, sinful self is dead.
2. **Jesus Christ's Burial:** Submersion beneath the water symbolizes the burial of our old life.
3. **Jesus Christ's Resurrection:** Rising out of the water symbolizes stepping into a resurrected, new life as a child of God.

---

## C. Holy Communion

### 1. Meaning & Scriptural Basis
Holy Communion—also called the **Lord's Supper**, **Lord's Table**, or **Breaking of Bread**—was instituted by Jesus on the night He was betrayed.

> *"...that the Lord Jesus on the same night in which He was betrayed took bread; and when He had given thanks, He broke it and said, 'Take, eat; this is My body which is broken for you: do this in remembrance of Me.' In the same manner He also took the cup after supper, saying, 'This cup is the new covenant in My blood. This do, as often as you drink it, in remembrance of Me.' For as often as you eat this bread and drink this cup, you proclaim the Lord's death till He comes."* — **1 Corinthians 11:23b–26**

#### The Two Emblems:
- **Bread (Jesus' Body):** Symbolizes Christ's body broken on the cross, bearing the punishment for our sins.
- **Wine / Juice (Jesus' Blood):** Symbolizes Christ's blood shed for the complete cleansing of our sins.

Partaking of Communion is an act of remembrance through which believers commemorate Christ's sacrifice and proclaim His death until He returns. In church today, Communion forms an integral part of regular Sunday worship services.

---

### 2. Who May Partake of Communion?
Since Communion commemorates what Jesus has done for a person's life, it is reserved for **born-again Christians** who have accepted Jesus Christ as their Lord and Savior and are willing to publicly confess their faith.

---

### 3. Proper Attitudes When Partaking

1. **Gratefulness (**Matthew 26:27**):** Expressing deep heartfelt thanks for Christ's sacrifice on the cross.
2. **Unity (**1 Corinthians 10:17**):** Demonstrating oneness and fellowship as fellow members of the Body of Christ.
3. **Self-Examination (**1 Corinthians 11:28–29**):**
   > *"So let a man examine himself, and so let him eat of the bread and drink of the cup. For he who eats and drinks in an unworthy manner eats and drinks judgment to himself, not discerning the Lord's body."* — **1 Corinthians 11:28–29**
   
   Believers must examine their hearts to confess any unrepentant sin before partaking. Failing to examine oneself brings God's judgment.

---

# Chapter 9: Baptism in the Holy Spirit

**Source:** *New Life In Christ: A Discipleship Guide for New Believers* (Pages 39–46)

---

## Introduction

As a new believer in Jesus, one of the most exciting promises given to us is found in **Acts 1:5**:
> *"For John baptized with water, but in a few days you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit."*

The baptism of the Holy Spirit is Jesus' promise to all believers to enable them to have the power to do the work of God effectively.

The promise of the Holy Spirit was given through the Bible and was directed to all people. It is God's desire that all of His children be filled (baptized) with the Spirit.

> *"For the promise is unto you, and to your children, and to all that are afar off, even as many as the Lord our God shall call."* — **Acts 2:39**

---

## The Promise

### a) Promised by God
- **God the Father** has promised the Holy Spirit (**Luke 11:13**).
- **Jesus** also promised the Holy Spirit (**John 14:16–17**).
- The Holy Spirit was promised in the **Old Testament** (**Joel 2:28**).
- He was also promised in the **New Testament**. Each writer of a Gospel made it a point to record this promise (**Matthew 3:1–12; Mark 1:8; Luke 3:16; John 1:33; Acts 1:8**).

### b) The Promise Was for God's People
- It is for **all people** (**Joel 2:28**).
- Paul desired for everyone to minister by the Spirit. First, he wanted them to speak in tongues and then that they would prophesy by the Spirit (**1 Corinthians 14:5**).

---

## The Purpose of the Gift

The purpose is to glorify Jesus:
> *"He will bring glory to me by taking from what is mine and making it known to you."* — **John 16:14**

### a) For the Purpose of Witness
> *"You shall receive power...to be witnesses...,"* — **Acts 1:8**

- The Holy Spirit convicts the world of sin (**John 16:8**).
- On the day Christians first received the Holy Spirit, 3,000 new people were added to the church (**Acts 2:41**).
- A great revival broke out in Ephesus after twelve were baptized in the Spirit (**Acts 19:6–10**).
- The Spirit gives us direction on where to go and what to say (**Acts 8:29, 10:19–20**).

### b) For the Purpose of Cleansing
- God's holiness is pictured as a consuming fire (**Hebrews 12:29**).
- Jesus will baptize with the Holy Spirit and fire (**Matthew 3:11–12**).
- Tongues of fire appeared on the day of Pentecost (**Acts 2:1–4**).
- It is by walking in the Spirit that we daily overcome sin (**Romans 8:1–4**).

### c) For the Purpose of Communicating
- Supernatural help will flow in prayer (**Romans 8:26; Jude 20**).
- You will experience deeper worship (**Ephesians 5:18–19; 1 Corinthians 14:15–16**).
- The Spirit will illuminate scripture (**John 14:26; 1 Thessalonians 1:5**).

---

## The Fruit & Gifts

The Holy Spirit works out His character and abilities in the believer's life. As you grow in Christ, you will see His character, "fruit", and gifts of ministry worked out. Some of the fruits and gifts appear in lists in the Bible. This is just a sample of what you can look for:

### a) Fruit
Nine (9) fruits of the Spirit are listed in **Galatians 5:22–23**:
1. Love
2. Joy
3. Peace
4. Longsuffering
5. Temperance (Self-control)
6. Gentleness
7. Goodness
8. Faith
9. Meekness

### b) Gifts
Nine (9) "gifts" are mentioned in **1 Corinthians 12:8–11**:
1. The word of wisdom
2. The word of knowledge
3. Faith
4. Gifts of healing
5. The working of miracles
6. Prophecy
7. Discerning of Spirits
8. Different kinds of tongues
9. Interpretation of tongues

---

## How to Receive the Holy Spirit

The Lord prepares us for the baptism in the Holy Spirit through sanctification, which is an ongoing process throughout our Christian journey. Baptism is a free gift that is not earned, and is promised to all. All that is required is that you **ASK** God to baptize you:

> *"If you then, though you are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him!"* — **Luke 11:13**

Receiving the baptism in the Holy Spirit does not depend on feelings, even though at times the recipient may be able to feel something happening inside of them. Rather, it is an act of faith, and as such, the recipient should not wait until they feel something. Once the believer has asked to be baptized by the Holy Spirit, like anything else we ask for, we should:

- **Ask for it.** (**Luke 11:9–13**)
- **Believe that God will give it.** (**James 1:6–7**)
- **Open your mouth and speak.** (**1 Corinthians 14:2**)
- **Test the spirit.** (**1 John 4:2–3**)

---

## Evidence and Benefits

### i) What evidence is there that one has been Baptized by the Holy Spirit?

Once a believer is baptized by the Holy Spirit, there is a manifestation of the gifts of the Spirit in their life.

#### a) Tongues
Many people in the Bible who were baptized by the Holy Spirit were able to speak in tongues. They were able to speak by faith as the Lord gave utterance. When a believer has asked to be baptized in the Holy Spirit, it is advisable for them to begin to open their mouth and let any new language that is within them flow out. In some cases, the believer hears the new sounds in their mind, but unless they open their mouth to speak, the sound will not be manifested through their voice.

> *"And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance."* — **Acts 2:4**

> *"...because the gift of the Holy Spirit had been poured out on the Gentiles also. For they heard them speak with tongues and magnify God."* — **Acts 10:45–46**

> *"And when Paul laid hands on them, the Holy Spirit came upon them, and they spoke with tongues and prophesied."* — **Acts 19:6**

#### b) Boldness
> *"And when they had prayed, the place where they were assembled together was shaken; and they were all filled with the Holy Spirit, and they spoke the word of God with boldness."* — **Acts 4:31**

#### c) Signs and Wonders
> *"And Stephen, full of faith and power, did great wonders and signs among the people."* — **Acts 6:8**

> *"And through the hands of the apostles many signs and wonders were done among the people."* — **Acts 5:12**

> *"Now God worked unusual miracles by the hands of Paul, so that even handkerchiefs or aprons were brought from his body to the sick, and the diseases left them and the evil spirits went out of them."* — **Acts 19:11–12**

> *"And believers were increasingly added to the Lord, multitudes of both men and women, so that they brought the sick out into the streets and laid them on beds and couches, that at least the shadow of Peter passing by might fall on some of them."* — **Acts 5:14–15**

---

### ii) What are the Benefits of Speaking in Tongues?

- **Tongues enable us to speak to God** – *"For he who speaks in tongues, speaks to God."* (**1 Corinthians 14:2**)
- **Tongues reveal to us the mysteries of God** – *"However in the spirit he speaks mysteries."* (**1 Corinthians 14:2**)
- **Tongues edify (build up) the believer** – *"He who speaks in tongues edifies himself."* (**1 Corinthians 14:4**)
- **Tongues are a weapon of spiritual warfare** – (**Ephesians 6:10–18**, specifically **v18**) *"Praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit."*
- **Tongues with interpretation are a sign to unbelievers** – *"Therefore tongues are for a sign, not to those who believe but to unbelievers."* (**1 Corinthians 14:22**)

---

### iii) Hindrances to Receiving the Baptism of the Holy Spirit

#### A) Fear
The Bible tells us to fear God only, for when you fear Him, you will have no need to fear anyone or anything else. *"The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, and knowledge of the Holy One is understanding."* (**Proverbs 9:10**). The children of God can approach the throne of grace with boldness because of Jesus Christ.

> *"Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need"* — **Hebrews 4:16**

There is no place for fear of rejection when seeking the baptism in the Holy Spirit, for your Heavenly Father delights in giving good gifts to His children.

> *"For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind"* — **2 Timothy 1:7**

#### B) Doubt / Unbelief
Doubt and unbelief prevent many people from entering into the promises God has for them. Remember that doubt stopped an entire generation of Israelites from entering into the Promised Land that God had given them; they died in the wilderness. Such is the experience of many Christians today.

> *"And without faith it is impossible to please God, because anyone who comes to him must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who earnestly seek him."* — **Hebrews 11:6**

> *"Consequently, faith comes from hearing the message, and the message is heard through the word of Christ."* — **Romans 10:17**

> *"If you then, though you are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him!"* — **Luke 11:13**

#### C) Wrong Teaching
Christians who accept wrong teaching on Holy Spirit baptism and speaking in tongues construct spiritual walls that keep them captive. The most common lies taught by some churches are:
- Speaking in tongues is demonic.
- Speaking in tongues is only for some Christians, not all.
- Holy Spirit Baptism happens automatically when one is born again.

#### D) Feelings of Inadequacy
No one can earn or deserve this gift. It is freely given to those who have been made righteous by faith in Jesus Christ (**Ephesians 1:3**). Take your eyes off your own worthiness and place them on Him.

#### E) Sin
Sin separates us from God. Unconfessed sin is a major hindrance to receiving the Holy Spirit and speaking in tongues.

> *"If I had not confessed the sin in my heart, my Lord would not have listened. But God did listen! He paid attention to my prayer."* — **Psalm 66:18**

#### F) Unforgiveness
Nothing clogs the flow of the Holy Spirit like bitterness. It makes no difference whether or not you feel you have a "right" to be bitter. Be totally honest with yourself. There may be people you've rejected for so long that it seems normal. Forgive them in your heart, and take any necessary steps to reconcile (**Mark 11:25; Matthew 5:23–24**).

# Chapter 10: Sharing Your Faith

## A. What Does It Mean to Share Your Faith?

As Christians, we are commanded to share with others about our faith in our Lord Jesus Christ. Having experienced His goodness and the gift of eternal life, we share this Good News so that others may also discover eternal life in Jesus.

## B. Why Do We Need to Share Our Faith?

We share with others so that they may also trust in Jesus Christ for their salvation because:

1. **John 14:6** — People **cannot** be saved any other way. Most people believe that by being good people and doing good works, they can earn their way to heaven. The Bible clearly states that this is not so.
2. **1 Timothy 2:3–5** — It is God's desire that **everyone** be saved. God does not want any to go to hell.
3. **Matthew 28:19–20** — Jesus **commands** that we witness for Him. Jesus commissioned the early disciples to be witnesses for Him, and He commissions us to do the same today.
4. **Proverbs 11:30; 1 Thessalonians 2:19** — There are **eternal rewards** for those who win souls. There are rewards in heaven for those who lead others to Christ.

## C. How Do We Share Our Faith?

### 1. Friendship Building
- **Daily Life Conversation:** Build natural rapport by talking about common interests, family, work, or daily life.
- **Spiritual Background:** Listen attentively and inquire about their upbringing, beliefs, or faith background.
- **Spiritual Activities:** Share naturally what you enjoy about church, small groups, or your spiritual journey.
- **Launch Questions:** Gently pivot the conversation from everyday topics toward deeper spiritual discussions.
- **Bridge Question:** Ask, *"On a scale of 1–10, how fulfilled do you think your life is?"* before transitioning into your personal testimony.

### 2. Diagnostic Questions
- **Diagnostic Question 1:** *Do you know for sure that you would be with God in Heaven?*
- **Diagnostic Question 2:** *Why should God let you into His Heaven?*

> *"I have good news for you!"*

### 3. The 5 Fingers Gospel Outline (G-R-A-C-E)

#### **G — GRACE**
1. **Eternal life is a gift of God**
   > *" ...the gift of God is eternal life in our Lord Jesus Christ."* — **Romans 6:23b**
2. **It is not earned or deserved**
   > *"For it is by grace you have been saved through faith; and this is not from yourself, it is the gift of God, not by works, so that no one can boast."* — **Ephesians 2:8–9**
- **Illustration:** *A Friend's Gift* — Eternal life is a free gift; it cannot be earned or purchased, just as you would not pay a friend for a birthday present.
- **Transition Question:** *What hinders us from receiving this gift? Sin.*

#### **M — MAN**
1. **Man is a sinner**
   > *"All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God."* — **Romans 3:23**
   - *Dialogue:* What is sin?
   - **Illustration:** *Three Sins a Day* — Even a "good" person committing just 3 small sins a day accumulates over 1,000 sins a year—showing our deep need for forgiveness before God.
2. **Man cannot save himself**
   - *Past Understanding:* Man's self-directed attempt to earn salvation through good works.
   - *Dialogue:* How good is good enough?
   > *"Be perfect, therefore, as your heavenly Father is perfect."* — **Matthew 5:48**
   - **Illustration:** *Rotten Egg* — Just as one bad egg ruins an entire omelet, a single sin contaminates all human good works before a holy God. It is impossible for man to save himself.
- **Transition:** *Let's look at God's way.*

#### **G — GOD**
- *Dialogue:* What are the characteristics of God?
1. **Misconceptions about God:**
   - *Grandfather figure:* Loving but not just.
   - *Policeman figure:* Just but not loving.
2. **God is merciful (loving) and just:**
   - **Love:** Jeremiah 31:3 — *"I have loved you with an everlasting love."*
   - **Just:** Exodus 34:7b — *"...Yet he does not leave the guilty unpunished."*
   - **Illustration:** *Bank Robber* — A judge who lets a bank robber go without punishment is unjust; similarly, God's perfect justice requires that sin be punished (Romans 6:23a: *"The wages of sin is death"*).
   - **Dilemma:** How can God's mercy and justice be shown at the same time?
   - **Illustration:** *Shamila* — Just as a king takes the physical punishment due to his guilty subject out of love, God fulfilled His justice and love by taking our penalty in Christ.

#### **C — CHRIST**
1. **Jesus is God who became man**
   > *"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."* — **John 1:1**
   > *"The Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us."* — **John 1:14**
2. **What did He do?**
   - He died on the cross to pay the penalty for our sins, rose from the dead, ascended to Heaven, and now offers us the gift of eternal life.
- **Transition:** *This gift of eternal life can be received through faith.*

#### **F — FAITH**
- **Illustration:** *Key* — Only the right key (Saving Faith) unlocks the door of Heaven; counterfeit or similar-looking keys (Non-saving faith) cannot open it.
1. **Non-Saving Faith:**
   - *Blind leap in the dark:* Believes blindly without knowing who or what is believed in.
   - *Head knowledge only:* Intellectually agrees with biblical facts about Jesus, but does not put personal trust in Him.
   - *Temporary faith:* Trusts Jesus only for temporal or physical needs in this life, but not for eternal salvation.
2. **Saving Faith:** Knowing and trusting in Jesus alone as Lord and Savior for eternal life.
   - **Illustration:** *Blondin* — Believing a tightrope walker can push a wheelbarrow across Niagara Falls is intellectual head knowledge; stepping inside the wheelbarrow yourself is saving faith.
   - **Illustration:** *Rescue Boat* — To be saved from a sinking vessel, you must step into the rescue boat and let go of whatever else you were clinging to.
   > *"I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father, except through Me."* — **John 14:6**

### 4. Closing & Leading to Commitment
- **Question 3:** *Does this all make sense to you?*
- **Question 4:** *Would you want to receive this gift of eternal life?*

#### **Sample Prayer:**
> *"Lord Jesus Christ, I know I am a sinner and do not deserve eternal life. But I believe You died and rose from the grave to purchase a place in heaven for me. Lord Jesus, come into my life; take control of my life; forgive my sins and save me. I repent of my sins now and place my trust in You for my salvation. I accept the free gift of eternal life. Amen."*

## D. What If the Person Declines?

If the person you are sharing the gospel with refuses to accept Jesus Christ, remember:
1. It is a fact that not everybody will want to accept Jesus Christ as their Lord and Savior.
2. It is **not you** they are rejecting; it is Jesus they are rejecting. Do not take the rejection personally.
3. Responding with kindness keeps the door open, whereas an unkind reaction can push them away from Christ.
4. **Never forget to continue praying** for the person.
