# Comprehensive Study: The Five Objections of Moses (Exodus 3:11–4:17)

## Exegetical Analysis and Source Material

In **Exodus 3:11–4:17**, the narrative records the profound dialogue between God and Moses at Mount Horeb (the burning bush). When God commissions Moses to deliver the children of Israel out of Egyptian bondage, Moses responds by raising **five consecutive objections**. 

This 5-part dialogue is a cornerstone of classic Old Testament exegesis, expounded across historic commentaries (e.g., *Jamieson-Fausset-Brown Commentary*, *Matthew Henry's Commentary*, *Keil and Delitzsch Biblical Commentary on the Old Testament*).

---

### The 5 Objections, Root Causes, and Divine Responses

| # | Moses' Objection | Scripture Reference | Root Fear / Excuse | God's Divine Answer & Revelation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Unworthiness / Identity**<br>*"Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh and bring the Israelites out of Egypt?"* | **Exodus 3:11** | Insecurity, past failure (killing the Egyptian in Exod 2), feeling insignificant as an obscure shepherd in Midian. | **The Promise of Divine Presence** (Exod 3:12):<br>*“I will surely be with you...”* God shifts the focus away from Moses' qualifications to God's presence. |
| **2** | **Authority / Knowledge**<br>*"Suppose I go... and they ask me, ‘What is His name?’ What should I tell them?"* | **Exodus 3:13** | Fear of lacking divine authority, credentials, and theological standing before Israel's elders. | **The Revelation of the Covenant Name** (Exod 3:14–15):<br>*“I AM WHO I AM... I AM has sent me to you.”* God reveals His eternal, self-existent covenant Name (*Yahweh*). |
| **3** | **Credibility / Rejection**<br>*"What if they refuse to believe me or listen to my voice? For they may say, ‘The LORD has not appeared to you.’"* | **Exodus 4:1** | Fear of skepticism and rejection by his peers, recalling how Israel rejected his intervention 40 years earlier (Exod 2:14). | **The Provision of Three Validating Signs** (Exod 4:2–9):<br>1. Shepherd's staff turning into a serpent.<br>2. Hand turned leprous and restored.<br>3. Nile water turned to blood on dry ground. |
| **4** | **Inadequacy / Ineloquence**<br>*"Please, Lord, I have never been eloquent... for I am slow of speech and tongue."* | **Exodus 4:10** | Fear of personal speech limitations and lack of diplomatic polish before Pharaoh's court. | **The Sovereignty of the Creator** (Exod 4:11–12):<br>*“Who gave man his mouth?... Now go! I will help you as you speak, and I will teach you what to say.”* |
| **5** | **Reluctance / Outright Evasion**<br>*"Please, Lord, send someone else."* (*Shelach-na beyad-tishlach*) | **Exodus 4:13** | Pure resistance and unwillingness to obey; exposing that previous reasons were excuses masking reluctance. | **Righteous Anger & Gracious Provision** (Exod 4:14–17):<br>The Lord's anger burns at Moses' stubbornness, yet in mercy He appoints Aaron the Levite as spokesman and confirms Moses' authority with the staff. |

### Source Material & Commentator Insights

1. **Jamieson, Fausset, and Brown (JFB Commentary on Exodus 3–4)**:
   * JFB notes that Moses' initial humility (*"Who am I?"*) was natural after 40 years of wilderness obscurity, but quickly degenerated into distrust of God's accompanying power. On Exodus 4:13, JFB comments that Moses was anxious to be freed from personal responsibility, but God graciously overcame every remaining excuse by sending Aaron to be his colleague.
2. **Matthew Henry's Commentary**:
   * Henry notes that Moses' five objections progressed from modesty to infirmity, and finally to sinful reluctance. He emphasizes that when God answers *"I will surely be with you"*, it establishes that God's sufficiency is greater than human weakness.
3. **Keil & Delitzsch Biblical Commentary**:
   * Highlights the Hebrew idiom in Exodus 4:13 (*"Send by the hand of whomever You will send"*), showing that Moses exhausted all intellectual and practical rationalizations until only his bare reluctance remained.

---

### Theological Lessons for Leadership

1. **God Does Not Call the Qualified; He Qualifies the Called**: Divine leadership does not depend on self-sufficiency, public speaking prowess, or personal charisma, but on the presence and power of God.
2. **Excuses Mask Unbelief**: The progression from Exodus 3:11 to 4:13 demonstrates that endless excuses about our limitations often mask an unwillingness to surrender control to God.
3. **God's Presence is the Ultimate Answer**: God never answered Moses' objections by inflating his ego or boosting his self-confidence; instead, God continually pointed to His own character, sovereignty, and promises.
