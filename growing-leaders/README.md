## Project purpose

Growing Leaders is a six-session introduction to the Christian leadership journey for new and emerging leaders. It is an accessible offshoot of the more extensive *Leadership Emergence Journey* and is designed for informal mentoring rather than a classroom or academic course.

The project helps participants recognise that God forms the whole leader over time. Leadership development involves what a person understands, who they are becoming, how they serve, and the practices that shape their daily life.

The four dimensions of formation are:

- **Head — leadership understanding:** biblical knowledge, leadership principles, and an understanding of how God develops leaders.
- **Heart — inner formation:** character, integrity, humility, motives, and love for God and people.
- **Hands — leadership practice:** leadership and relational skills, faithful service, and the discovery and development of spiritual gifts.
- **Habits — repeated formation:** small, intentional leadership practices that turn insight and conviction into a way of life.

These dimensions are integrated in every session. Habits are not an additional topic or a separate course section; they help participants consistently embody what they are learning in their head, heart, and hands.

## Audience

The primary audience is new and emerging Christian leaders who:

- are beginning to serve or take responsibility in church, ministry, work, community, or other relational settings;
- may have little formal leadership training;
- benefit from clear, conversational, and practical material;
- are willing to reflect honestly, participate in a small mentoring group, and practise what they learn;
- want to grow in their relationship with God as well as in leadership ability.

The material is intended for a facilitator mentoring two or three participants. It may also be adapted for similarly small groups where trust, participation, and personal attention are possible.

## Core objectives

By the end of the six sessions, participants should be able to:

1. Understand leadership development as an ongoing process of formation, not merely the acquisition of techniques or a position.
2. Recognise how their background, experiences, responsibilities, relationships, pressures, successes, and failures can contribute to their development.
3. Identify character issues, motives, attitudes, and relational patterns that affect the way they lead.
4. Practise foundational leadership and relational skills in real situations.
5. Begin recognising and developing their strengths and spiritual gifts through service, fruit, feedback, and reflection.
6. Receive feedback, correction, responsibility, and difficulty with greater humility and maturity.
7. Develop small, sustainable habits that support faithful leadership and continued growth.
8. Form a realistic next step for their continued development with God and within community.

## Goals and intended outcomes

The course aims to form leaders who:

- lead from an increasing love for God and people;
- value character and faithfulness more than position, recognition, or influence;
- understand themselves more honestly without becoming self-absorbed;
- accept responsibility and follow through reliably;
- relate to people with humility, courage, care, and appropriate boundaries;
- treat feedback and difficulty as opportunities for learning;
- use their strengths and gifts to serve rather than establish status;
- reflect on their experience and respond to God;
- translate learning into consistent practice through healthy leadership habits;
- remain teachable and committed to lifelong formation.

The measure of success is not simply that participants complete the reading or remember the ideas. Success is demonstrated when they notice what God may be forming, respond honestly, practise a concrete action, receive feedback, and continue growing.

## Six-session content

The proposed journey is:

| Session | Main emphasis | Key takeaway |
|---|---|---|
| **1. God Uses Your Story** | Background, formative experiences, patterns, strengths, pain, and healing | God can use both the good and difficult parts of your story as you grow. |
| **2. God Forms Your Character** | Integrity, responsiveness to Scripture, obedience, motives, failure, repair, and accountability | Pressure reveals where growth is needed, and God’s Word guides a faithful response. |
| **3. God Builds Your Faithfulness** | Responsibility, service, reliability, feedback, recognition, and healthy limits | God builds trustworthy leaders through repeated acts of ordinary faithfulness. |
| **4. God Develops Your Gifts** | Natural strengths, learned skills, spiritual gifts, fruit, practice, and community confirmation | Gifts and strengths become clearer and more useful through service, feedback, and development. |
| **5. God Shapes How You Lead People** | Listening, authority, correction, conflict, criticism, relationships, and boundaries | Leadership maturity is revealed in the way a leader treats people, especially when relationships are difficult. |
| **6. God Deepens Your Life With Him** | Dependence on God, spiritual depth, sustainability, integration, values, and continued development | Sustainable leadership flows from a deepening life with God, humility, learning, and healthy relationships. |

Each session addresses Head, Heart, Hands, and Habits in relation to its central theme.

## Learning approach

Growing Leaders follows a repeated movement:

> **Understand → Examine → Respond → Practise → Share → Commit**

### Before the mentoring conversation

Participants complete approximately 20–30 minutes of personal preparation. The material should help them:

1. Enter the subject through a familiar leadership situation or story.
2. Understand one clear biblical leadership principle.
3. Engage carefully with one main Scripture passage.
4. Connect the principle with ordinary leadership situations.
5. Reflect through the Head, Heart, Hands, and Habits framework.
6. Respond personally to God.
7. Choose a specific action or habit to practise.
8. Prepare one insight, one honest reflection, and one intended response to share.

Personal preparation should generate material for conversation rather than answer every question or cover the subject comprehensively.

### During the mentoring conversation

Each session includes approximately one hour of facilitated discussion with two or three participants. A typical conversation follows this rhythm:

| Time | Focus |
|---|---|
| 0–5 minutes | Arrive, reconnect, and pray |
| 5–15 minutes | Review the previous practice and habit |
| 15–25 minutes | Share what stood out from the reading and Scripture |
| 25–40 minutes | Explore understanding, experience, motives, and personal application |
| 40–50 minutes | Practise a leadership or relational skill together |
| 50–57 minutes | Make one specific commitment for the coming week |
| 57–60 minutes | Pray for one another |

The group discussion should deepen and apply the personal reading rather than repeat or lecture through it. Participants should speak, reflect, and practise more than the facilitator teaches.

## Head, Heart, Hands, and Habits framework

Every session should make the four dimensions explicit.

### Head: What do I need to understand?

This dimension presents one memorable biblical leadership principle and helps participants examine assumptions about leadership.

Typical prompts include:

- What am I learning about God, leadership, people, or myself?
- What assumption is being confirmed or challenged?
- Why does this principle matter in real leadership?

### Heart: Who am I becoming?

This dimension attends to character, desires, fears, motives, relationships, and responsiveness to God.

Typical prompts include:

- What is this situation revealing within me?
- Where might pride, fear, insecurity, resentment, or people-pleasing be influencing me?
- What do I need to receive, confess, surrender, repair, or trust?

### Hands: What do I need to practise?

This dimension turns the principle into leadership and relational action.

Typical prompts include:

- What conversation, decision, or act of service is needed?
- What skill should I practise?
- From whom should I seek help or feedback?
- What would faithful action look like in my present responsibility?

### Habits: What will help this become a way of life?

This dimension gives participants one small and repeatable practice connected to the session. A habit should reinforce the session's Head, Heart, and Hands learning rather than sit alongside them as an unrelated activity.

Each habit should define:

- **Purpose:** why the habit matters for formation and leadership;
- **Cue:** when, where, or in response to what the participant will act;
- **Practice:** the small and observable behaviour to repeat;
- **Review:** how the participant will notice what happened and what was learned;
- **Support:** who will encourage, remind, or give feedback;
- **Continuation:** whether the habit should be retained, adjusted, or replaced after the session.

Habits must be realistic enough to practise during ordinary life. They are experiments in formation, not tests of worthiness or spirituality. In the following session, participants review what they attempted, what happened, and what they learned, including when the practice did not go as planned.

## Suggested leadership habits

| Session | Habit | Weekly practice |
|---|---|---|
| **1. God Uses Your Story** | Notice and Learn | Review a significant experience, notice what happened within you, and consider what it may be revealing or teaching you. |
| **2. God Forms Your Character** | Pause and Bring It Before God | Pause under pressure, then Notice, Name, Receive, and Respond before God. |
| **3. God Builds Your Faithfulness** | Record and Follow Through | Capture commitments, review them daily, and complete or communicate them. |
| **4. God Develops Your Gifts** | Serve, Notice, Ask | Practise a possible gift, notice the fruit, and ask for specific feedback. |
| **5. God Shapes How You Lead People** | Listen Before Responding | Understand and summarise the other person's perspective before offering your own. |
| **6. God Deepens Your Life With Him** | Abide and Review | Remain attentive to God while reviewing your leadership and choosing a faithful next step. |

The habits form a developmental progression:

> **Notice → Receive → Follow Through → Serve → Listen → Abide**

## Facilitator approach

The facilitator serves as a mentor, guide, model, and attentive questioner rather than primarily as a lecturer.

The facilitator should:

- create a safe, prayerful, and appropriately confidential environment;
- invite honesty without forcing disclosure;
- listen for significant experiences, emotions, assumptions, patterns, and invitations from God;
- use open questions and patient follow-up rather than providing immediate answers;
- share personally and appropriately without making the session about themselves;
- ensure every participant has space to contribute;
- connect biblical truth with the participant's actual responsibilities and relationships;
- help participants make small, specific, and reviewable commitments;
- give encouragement and challenge with grace and clarity;
- revisit previous commitments and habits without creating shame;
- recognise situations that require pastoral, safeguarding, or professional support beyond the group's purpose.

As a general guide, the facilitator should speak for no more than about one-third of the meeting.

## Content principles

Each session should:

- be centred on one main idea and one main Scripture passage;
- begin with life and experience before introducing explanation;
- be biblically responsible, spiritually formative, and practically useful;
- integrate Head, Heart, Hands, and Habits;
- include original examples, questions, exercises, and applications;
- make space for both encouragement and honest self-examination;
- connect individual growth with service, relationships, and community;
- include one practical activity or leadership experiment;
- conclude with one specific next step;
- build on earlier sessions without requiring academic knowledge.

## Tone and writing style

The participant material should be:

- **conversational:** written as a trusted mentor speaking with a new leader;
- **clear:** using ordinary language and explaining any necessary term;
- **warm:** communicating grace, hope, and confidence in God's formative work;
- **honest:** acknowledging weakness, failure, pain, conflict, and mixed motives;
- **reflective:** creating space to notice, think, pray, and respond;
- **practical:** consistently connecting ideas with real responsibilities and relationships;
- **inviting rather than condemning:** encouraging responsibility without using shame;
- **substantive but not academic:** offering meaningful insight without unnecessary complexity;
- **humble:** avoiding simplistic claims about what God is doing in a person's circumstances.

The material should not sound like a sermon transcript, leadership textbook, motivational speech, or condensed version of another author's work.

## Scope and constraints

Growing Leaders is intentionally limited in scope.

- It is a six-session introduction, not a comprehensive leadership curriculum.
- Each session supports about 20–30 minutes of personal preparation and one hour of group conversation.
- The group is designed for one facilitator and two or three participants.
- Every session should prioritise depth and application over breadth of content.
- Participants should normally receive one main principle, one main passage, one primary practice, and one leadership habit per session.
- Exercises and commitments should be possible within participants' existing responsibilities; the course should not require them to hold a formal leadership title.
- Personal reflection is encouraged, but participants should not be pressured to disclose private or painful experiences.
- The course is not counselling, trauma therapy, formal spiritual direction, or a substitute for pastoral care.
- Facilitators must observe appropriate confidentiality, safeguarding, organisational, and legal responsibilities.
- Difficult leadership situations should not be framed as automatic proof of God's approval, a participant's failure, or a particular divine intention.

## Originality and source use

The course may be informed by broad insights from J. Robert Clinton's *The Making of a Leader* and the wider Leadership Emergence Journey. However, Growing Leaders must remain an original, audience-specific course rather than a condensed reproduction of another work.

Accordingly:

- explanations should be written independently in fresh language;
- distinctive diagrams, tables, sequences, and extended passages from source works should not be reproduced;
- examples, reflection questions, exercises, and the Head, Heart, Hands, and Habits framework should be original;
- direct quotations should be brief, necessary, attributed, and properly referenced;
- sources should be acknowledged as influences and recommended for further reading where appropriate;
- the course should not be presented as officially approved by any author or publisher.

## Project standard

Every element of Growing Leaders should serve the same aim: helping new leaders pay attention to how God is forming them and take a faithful next step.

A completed session should enable a participant to say:

> I understand one important truth more clearly. I have examined what it reveals in me. I know one action to practise. I have chosen one habit that can help this become part of the way I lead.
