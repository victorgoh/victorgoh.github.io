# Growing Leaders: Everyday Edition

> **A Practical 6-Module Mentoring Journey for Stepping Into Leadership at Church, Work, and Home**  
> *A grounded, practical approach to Christian leadership for adults balancing work, family, church, and community responsibilities.*

---

# Course Introduction

## I.1 Welcome to the Journey

Most adults never set out to become "leaders." 

Instead, leadership usually finds us in the middle of everyday life:
* You are asked to coordinate a volunteer team, lead a community initiative, or host a small group.
* Your workplace promotes you to manage projects, supervise colleagues, or train junior staff.
* You find yourself organizing family matters, caring for an aging parent, or guiding your children through difficult choices.

Whenever people depend on your care, trust your judgment, or look to you for direction, you are leading.

### The Question Beneath the Surface
When new responsibility lands on our shoulders, our first reaction is often a mixture of excitement and hesitation: *"Do I have enough time and energy for this?"* or *"What if I make a mistake?"*

It is natural to look for quick tips, techniques, and checklists. While practical skills certainly help, Christian leadership begins with a deeper, far more comforting truth:

> **God does not just care about what you get done. He cares deeply about who you are becoming as you serve.**

Leadership is one of the primary tools God uses to mature us. Your patience, your marriage, your integrity, your friendships, and your daily walk with God all grow together. This course is an invitation to notice how God is shaping you in the middle of ordinary life and to take faithful next steps.

---

## I.2 The 4 Areas of Growth

In every module, we explore four connected areas of life:

| Dimension | Key Question | What It Looks Like in Daily Life |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 🧠 **Head** | *What do I need to understand?* | Clear biblical principles that shape how you see God, people, and work. |
| ❤️ **Heart** | *Who am I becoming?* | Growing in inner maturity, humility, emotional health, and integrity under pressure. |
| ✋ **Hands** | *What do I need to put into practice?* | Practical communication, decision-making, and service skills you can use immediately. |
| 🔁 **Habits** | *What will help this stick?* | Small, realistic daily and weekly rhythms that keep you grounded over the long haul. |

---

### The 6 Core Habits

Across the six modules, you will build six simple, sustainable habits:

1. **Reflect and Grow:** A 3-minute evening review to discern where God was at work and extract wisdom.
2. **Pause and Pray:** Taking a 30-second breath before reacting to pressure, stress, or conflict.
3. **Capture and Clarify:** Promptly recording commitments, clarifying expectations, and following through.
4. **Serve to Discover:** Stepping up in practical service and asking for feedback to discover and sharpen strengths.
5. **Understand before Solving:** Pausing, asking open questions, and summarising before giving advice or solutions.
6. **Abide to Grow:** Guarding unhurried daily time with Jesus and weekly aligning priorities with God.

---

## I.4 Preparing for Group Meetings (Designed for Busy Schedules)

We recognize that your week is already full with workplace deadlines, family needs, household responsibilities, and church commitments. 

This personal preparation is **not academic homework, an exam, or a corporate evaluation**. Think of it as a brief, protected breathing space for your soul: an unhurried conversation with God before you meet with your group.

### Three Practical Guidelines:
1. **Bullet Points Over Essays:** You do not need to write polished paragraphs. Quick notes, single words, or raw questions are completely sufficient.
2. **Focus on What Resonates:** If you are pressed for time, focus on the Scripture passage and the one or two questions that speak most directly to your current situation.
3. **You Control What You Share:** Everything you write in this guide is private between you and God. You decide what feels helpful and appropriate to share in group discussions.

### Two Simple Rhythms to Fit Your Week:
* **The 25-Minute Coffee Pause:** Set aside one quiet 25-minute window during the week with a warm drink, your Bible, and this guide.
* **The "Two-Bite" Rhythm (15 mins + 10 mins):**
  * *Bite 1 (Mid-week, 15 mins):* Read the opening scenario, core principle, and Scripture during your morning commute or lunch break.
  * *Bite 2 (Day before meeting, 10 mins):* Pray through the *Bring It Before God* prompts and pick one thought to bring to your group.

# Module 1: God Uses Your Story

> **Main Idea:** God uses your family background, career path, successes, and even your hardest seasons to shape the way you lead today. He redeems your story to give you deeper empathy, wisdom, and humility.  
> **Key Scripture:** Genesis 50:15–21 (Joseph’s perspective on his life)  
> **Leadership Habit:** *Reflect and Grow*

> *"You intended to harm me, but God intended it all for good. He brought me to this position so I could save the lives of many people."*  
> — **Genesis 50:20**

---

## 1. Real Life: The Baggage We Bring to the Table

Consider two team leaders in their 40s:

* **Andrew** grew up in a home where disagreements were resolved calmly around the kitchen table. When someone on his church volunteer team drops the ball, he picks up the phone, talks through the issue with patience, and helps them get back on track without creating drama.
* **David** grew up in an environment where mistakes were met with harsh criticism or tense, stony silence. When a team member misses an important deadline, David’s stomach knots up. He either takes over the job himself out of frustration or avoids talking to the person altogether, letting resentment build.

Neither Andrew nor David began with a clean slate. Both brought decades of learned habits, family patterns, and past workplace experiences into their leadership.

The same is true for all of us.

By the time we reach our 30s, 40s, or 50s, we carry a significant life story:
* The values, work ethic, and communication styles our parents modeled for us.
* Career successes that built our confidence.
* Painful disappointments, job redundancies, broken partnerships, or strained relationships that made us cautious, defensive, or hesitant to trust others.

Leadership inevitably brings these hidden patterns to the surface. When deadlines loom or people disagree, our default habits take over.

---

## 2. Core Truth: God Redeems Your History

God does not ask you to pretend your past never happened, nor does He tell you to leave your personality at the door. He meets you right inside your story.

Look at Joseph in the Book of Genesis:
* As a young man, his own brothers became jealous, betrayed him, and sold him into slavery.
* In Egypt, he worked with integrity as a servant and manager, only to be falsely accused and thrown into prison for years.
* Yet through every painful chapter, God was quietly forming Joseph's character, maturity, and capacity to govern with wisdom.

Years later, when Joseph was promoted to lead Egypt and held the power to take revenge on his brothers, he chose grace, forgiveness, and redemptive perspective instead:

> *"You intended to harm me, but God intended it all for good. He brought me to this position so I could save the lives of many people."* (Genesis 50:20)

God can take the hardest seasons of your life—an unfair workplace experience, a difficult season in your family, or a personal failure—and use them to give you deeper compassion, wisdom, and emotional maturity as you care for others.

---

## 3. The 4 Areas of Growth for Module 1

* 🧠 **Head (Clear Thinking):** My past experiences shape my present reactions. Recognizing where my habits come from helps me respond thoughtfully rather than reacting out of instinct.
* ❤️ **Heart (Inner Character):** I can bring old hurts, insecurities, and regrets to God instead of letting them secretly drive my leadership.
* ✋ **Hands (Practical Action):** When I feel unusually defensive, anxious, or irritated this week, I will pause and ask: *"Is my reaction really about this moment, or is it triggered by something from my past?"*
* 🔁 **Habit (Reflect and Grow):** At the end of the day, take 3 minutes to review one significant interaction or event. Ask: *What did this reveal about my heart, and what is God inviting me to learn?*

---

## 4. Bring It Before God

Take 5 unhurried minutes to pray through these four steps:

1. **Notice:** Think of one significant season from your past (a family dynamic, a career turning point, or a season of hardship) that still influences how you lead and relate to people today.
2. **Name:** Speak openly to God about how that season felt (grief, anger, pride, fear, relief, or gratitude).
3. **Receive:** Welcome God's healing truth and perspective. Remember that your identity is rooted in Christ, not in your past mistakes or achievements.
4. **Respond:** Ask: *"Lord, how can You use what I have walked through to encourage, protect, or support someone else today?"*

---

## 5. Group Discussion Questions

1. When you look back at your working life or family background, what is one positive habit or strength you are grateful to have inherited?
2. Under pressure or conflict, is your default tendency to *take over and control*, *withdraw and stay quiet*, or *appease everyone to keep the peace*? Where do you think that comes from?
3. How does Joseph’s story give you hope when you reflect on past setbacks or unfair circumstances you have experienced?

---

## 6. Small Step for the Week

Pick one interaction this week at work, church, or home where you usually feel impatient or defensive. Before responding, take a slow breath, remember who you are in Christ, and listen with an open mind.

# Module 2: God Forms Your Character

> **Main Idea:** True leadership is built on integrity—who you are when no one is watching and how you act under pressure. God uses daily challenges to build deep inner strength.  
> **Key Scripture:** Romans 5:1–5 & 1 Samuel 24:1–12 (David in the cave with Saul)  
> **Leadership Habit:** *Pause and Pray*

> *"We can rejoice, too, when we run into problems and trials, for we know that they help us develop endurance. And endurance develops strength of character, and character strengthens our confident hope of salvation."*  
> — **Romans 5:3–4**

---

## 1. Real Life: The Pressure Cooker

Think about a standard kitchen pressure cooker. Under high heat and sealed pressure, whatever is inside comes to a boil quickly.

Adult life often feels like a pressure cooker:
* Financial pressures and mortgage deadlines.
* Balancing demanding workplace targets with parenting or marriage needs.
* Difficult personalities in volunteer teams or committee meetings.
* Unexpected health concerns or fatigue.

When pressure mounts:
* **Insecurity** tempts us to overwork or seek everyone's approval.
* **Frustration** tempts us to speak sharply to colleagues, volunteers, or family members.
* **Fatigue** tempts us to cut moral or professional corners just to get through the day.

Pressure does not create what is in our hearts; it simply reveals what is already there.

---

## 2. Core Truth: Character Outweighs Reputation

The world focuses on **reputation** (what people say about you on LinkedIn or in public). God focuses on **character** (who you are when the doors are closed).

In 1 Samuel 24, David was hiding in a dark desert cave from King Saul, who was unjustly hunting him down. Saul walked right into the cave alone. David’s men whispered eagerly: *"This is your golden opportunity! Kill him right now, end your suffering, and take the throne!"*

David had the opportunity, the weapon, and the peer pressure to take a shortcut. But David cut only a corner of Saul’s robe, and his conscience immediately struck him. David refused to do the wrong thing to achieve a God-given promise:

> *"The Lord forbid that I should do this to my lord the king. I must not attack him, for he is the Lord’s anointed."* (1 Samuel 24:6)

David understood that **how you get to a position determines how you will lead in it**. Taking ungodly shortcuts damages our soul. God uses seasons in the wilderness to forge leaders who trust His timing rather than forcing their own way.

---

## 3. The 4 Areas of Growth for Module 2

* 🧠 **Head (Clear Thinking):** Trials and delays are not wasted time. God uses them like resistance training to build endurance and spiritual depth.
* ❤️ **Heart (Inner Character):** A commitment to choose honesty, humility, and patience even when taking a shortcut seems easier and faster.
* ✋ **Hands (Practical Action):** When I feel provoked or cornered, I will refuse to retaliate, gossip, or send an angry email in haste.
* 🔁 **Habit (Pause and Pray):** Whenever you feel your pulse quicken or frustration rise, pause for 10 seconds. Silently pray: *"Lord, grant me Your wisdom, patience, and self-control right now."*

---

## 4. Bring It Before God

1. **Notice:** When was the last time you felt stressed, unfairly treated, or tempted to cut a corner at work or home?
2. **Name:** Confess honestly to God what surfaced in your thoughts, words, or attitude.
3. **Receive:** Thank Jesus for His boundless grace. Ask the Holy Spirit to produce His fruit in you: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, and self-control.
4. **Respond:** If you spoke harshly, acted out of pride, or neglected an obligation, take the humble step to make it right with a sincere, straightforward apology.

---

## 5. Group Discussion Questions

1. When your schedule is overloaded or things go wrong, what is your most common warning sign that stress is taking over (e.g., irritability, withdrawal, sleeplessness, micromanagement)?
2. Why is taking a moral or relational shortcut so tempting when we are trying to solve a problem quickly?
3. How has a past trial or difficult season in your life helped you grow in patience or compassion?

---

## 6. Small Step for the Week

The next time you receive a frustrating message or encounter an annoying situation, wait at least one hour before sending a written response. Bring your initial reaction to God first.

# Module 3: God Builds Your Faithfulness

> **Main Idea:** Significant leadership is built on ordinary reliability. Being trustworthy with small, hidden responsibilities prepares you to steward greater influence.  
> **Key Scripture:** Luke 16:10–12 & Matthew 25:14–23 (The Parable of the Talents)  
> **Leadership Habit:** *Capture and Clarify*

> *"If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones. But if you are dishonest in little things, you won't be honest with greater responsibilities."*  
> — **Luke 16:10**

---

## 1. Real Life: The Hidden Cost of Dropped Balls

Imagine a colleague or church volunteer who is full of exciting ideas and charisma. They love speaking at meetings and starting new initiatives. 

However:
* They regularly arrive 15 minutes late.
* They promise to send important documents or follow up with a volunteer, but forget.
* They overcommit because they hate saying no, leaving other team members scrambling to clean up the mess.

Over time, despite their talent, people stop trusting them with important responsibilities.

Trust in adult life is built in drops and lost in buckets. Small commitments—showing up on time, following through on an unexciting task, keeping a confidential word—are the bricks with which leadership integrity is built.

---

## 2. Core Truth: God Values Faithful Stewards

In Jesus’ Parable of the Talents (Matthew 25), the master entrusted his resources to his servants while he went away. When he returned, his praise for the servants who invested their resources was simple and profound:

> *"Well done, my good and faithful servant. You have been faithful in handling this small amount, so now I will give you many more responsibilities. Let’s celebrate together!"* (Matthew 25:21)

Notice what the master rewarded:
* He did not reward popularity, flashiness, or charisma.
* He rewarded **faithfulness**—quiet, consistent, diligent stewardship over what had been entrusted to them.

Faithfulness as an adult leader means:
1. **Keeping your word:** Letting your "Yes" be yes and your "No" be no (James 5:12).
2. **Practicing healthy boundaries:** Saying a gracious *"No"* to good opportunities when your capacity is full, so that you can fulfill your existing commitments with excellence.
3. **Honoring the unseen tasks:** Managing rosters, preparing meeting agendas, setting up chairs, and caring for individuals behind the scenes with equal joy.

---

## 3. The 4 Areas of Growth for Module 3

* 🧠 **Head (Clear Thinking):** Competence without consistency destroys trust. Faithful stewardship in the small things proves character for greater responsibilities.
* ❤️ **Heart (Inner Character):** Finding genuine satisfaction in pleasing God through quiet, unheralded service rather than performing for public applause.
* ✋ **Hands (Practical Action):** Review your current commitments. If you are behind on a promise, communicate proactively and honestly today.
* 🔁 **Habit (Write It Down and Follow Through):** Maintain a single, reliable capture system (a notebook or phone task list). Whenever you make a commitment, record it immediately and review it daily.

---

## 4. Bring It Before God

1. **Notice:** Look across your current commitments at home, work, and church. Are there promises you have let slip?
2. **Name:** Talk to God about any tendencies toward procrastination, people-pleasing, or disorganization.
3. **Receive:** Ask God for the discipline, focus, and energy needed to honor your responsibilities with joy.
4. **Respond:** Choose one neglected task or overdue conversation and complete it with excellence within the next 48 hours.

---

## 5. Group Discussion Questions

1. Think of someone in your workplace or church whom you deeply trust. What specific habits make them so dependable?
2. Why is it often difficult for adults to say *"No"* to new requests, even when our schedules are already stretched to the limit?
3. What is one small, unglamorous responsibility in your current role that you can commit to doing with greater care and gratitude?

---

## 6. Small Step for the Week

Do an honest calendar check. Identify one commitment that you are currently dropping or neglecting. Either complete it promptly or have an honest, polite conversation to reset expectations.

# Module 4: God Develops Your Gifts

> **Main Idea:** God has equipped you with a distinct combination of natural abilities, learned skills, and spiritual gifts. You do not need to be good at everything; your role is to serve faithfully and help others thrive.  
> **Key Scripture:** 1 Peter 4:10–11 & Romans 12:3–8; Exodus 35:30–35  
> **Leadership Habit:** *Serve to Discover*

> *"God has given each of you a gift from his great variety of spiritual gifts. Use them well to serve one another. Do you have the gift of speaking? Then speak as though God himself were speaking through you. Do you have the gift of helping others? Do it with all the strength and energy that God supplies."*  
> — **1 Peter 4:10–11**

---

## 1. Real Life: The Comparison and Burnout Trap

In adult life, it is very easy to fall into one of two common gifting traps:

1. **The Comparison Trap:** You watch an articulate, visionary speaker or a highly organized administrator and think: *"I don't have those talents, so I must not have anything valuable to offer."*
2. **The "Do-It-All" Trap:** You see a need, feel responsible, and try to do everything yourself—from public speaking to budget spreadsheets to pastoral care. Within six months, you are exhausted, frustrated, and cynical.

Both traps arise from forgetting that God designed the Church as an interdependent body. No single individual is meant to carry every gift.

---

## 2. Core Truth: Stewarding God’s Diverse Grace

The Apostle Paul reminds us:

> *"Just as our bodies have many parts and each part has a special function, so it is with Christ’s body. We are many parts of one body, and we all belong to each other. In his grace, God has given us different gifts for doing certain things well."* (Romans 12:4–6)

Consider how gifts develop:
* **Gifts are discovered in motion:** You rarely discover your strengths by sitting alone taking personality tests. You discover them by rolling up your sleeves and serving real people.
* **Look for fruit and energy:** Where do people find genuine encouragement, clarity, or help through your service? What responsibilities give you energy rather than leaving you drained?
* **Embrace your limitations:** Knowing what you are *not* called or gifted to do is just as important as knowing your strengths. Great leaders surround themselves with people whose gifts complement their own weaknesses.

---

## 3. The 4 Areas of Growth for Module 4

* 🧠 **Head (Clear Thinking):** Leadership is a team sport. My responsibility is to contribute my strengths humbly while creating space for others to use theirs.
* ❤️ **Heart (Inner Character):** Security in Christ that celebrates other people's gifts without feeling threatened, envious, or insecure.
* ✋ **Hands (Practical Action):** Delegate or partner with someone on a task that falls outside your primary strengths, and encourage them as they step up.
* 🔁 **Habit (Serve to Discover):** Ask two trusted mentors or colleagues: *"Where do you see God using me most effectively to help others? What strengths do you notice?"*

---

## 4. Bring It Before God

1. **Notice:** What areas of serving give you deep joy and produce noticeable fruit in the lives of others?
2. **Name:** Confess any jealousy, comparison, or frustration where you have tried to operate outside your God-given grace.
3. **Receive:** Thank God for how He has uniquely designed your temperament, gifts, and life background.
4. **Respond:** Ask God to show you one person whose gifts you can encourage, mentor, or release into service this month.

---

## 5. Group Discussion Questions

1. When you look at your life and work, what are 1 or 2 areas where you feel most energized and effective when serving?
2. Have you ever tried to lead in an area that did not fit your gifts? What did that experience teach you about your limitations?
3. How can we encourage and build up each other’s gifts within our church or team instead of comparing ourselves?

# Module 5: God Shapes How You Lead People

> **Main Idea:** Christian leadership is defined by servant-hearted influence, not positional power. We lead people by listening with empathy, building mutual trust, and caring for their growth.  
> **Key Scripture:** Mark 10:42–45 & Philippians 2:1–11  
> **Leadership Habit:** *Understand before Solving*

> *"Whoever wants to be a leader among you must be your servant, and whoever wants to be first among you must be the slave of everyone. For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve others, and to give his life as a ransom for many."*  
> — **Mark 10:43–45**

---

## 1. Real Life: Positional Authority vs. Relational Trust

Think about two leaders you have worked under in your career or church life:

* **Leader 1 (Positional):** Relied on their title and rank. They gave orders, dominated discussions, rarely listened to concerns, and took credit for the team's successes while blaming others for failures. People did the bare minimum required when they were around.
* **Leader 2 (Relational & Servant):** Led with quiet humility and genuine respect. They asked insightful questions, listened carefully to feedback, backed their team up during challenges, and cared about the person behind the task. People went above and beyond to support them because they trusted their heart.

People do not follow a title for long; they follow leaders who genuinely care about them and their growth.

---

## 2. Core Truth: The Jesus Model of Leadership

In the ancient Roman world—and in our corporate world today—greatness is measured by how many people serve you, report to you, or applaud you.

Jesus completely upended this pyramid:

> *"You know that the rulers in this world lord it over their people, and officials flaunt their authority over those under them. But among you it will be different. Whoever wants to be a leader among you must be your servant."* (Mark 10:42–43)

Jesus demonstrated this not just in words, but by taking a towel, kneeling on the floor, and washing His disciples' dust-covered feet (John 13).

Leading people the Jesus way means:
1. **Listening to understand, not just to reply:** Hearing the unspoken fears, frustrations, and hopes beneath people’s words.
2. **Speaking the truth in love:** Giving constructive feedback with kindness and clarity, aiming for the person’s growth rather than proving yourself right.
3. **Using influence to lift others:** Using your position, experience, and resources to help others succeed, develop, and flourish.

| World's Hierarchy | Kingdom of God |
| :--- | :--- |
| *"Serve me; I am in charge."* | *"How can I help you grow?"* |
| Demands respect through title and position | Earns trust through humble care and service |
| Uses people to achieve personal goals | Uses influence to develop and build up people |

---

## 3. The 4 Areas of Growth for Module 5

* 🧠 **Head (Clear Thinking):** Authority is given for stewardship, service, and protection—never for self-advancement or control.
* ❤️ **Heart (Inner Character):** Cultivating genuine empathy, patience, and love for difficult people, seeing them as people made in God's image.
* ✋ **Hands (Practical Action):** In your next meeting or family discussion, ask clarifying questions (*"Help me understand your perspective on this"*) before giving your opinion.
* 🔁 **Habit (Understand before Solving):** Practice the "3-Second Rule." When someone finishes speaking, pause for three full seconds before you respond to ensure they feel heard.

# Module 6: God Deepens Your Life With Him

> **Main Idea:** Sustainable leadership flows out of a healthy, growing walk with Jesus. You cannot lead others where you have not walked with God yourself.  
> **Key Scripture:** John 15:1–8 (The Vine and the Branches) & Psalm 23  
> **Leadership Habit:** *Abide to Grow*

> *"Yes, I am the vine; you are the branches. Those who remain in me, and I in them, will produce much fruit. For apart from me you can do nothing."*  
> — **John 15:5**

---

## 1. Real Life: The Peril of Running on Empty

Think about an electric vehicle or a modern smartphone. No matter how powerful the engine or how advanced the apps, when the battery reaches zero, everything stops.

In the rush of adult responsibilities—career demands, parenting, community service, and church leadership—many well-meaning leaders make a dangerous mistake: **they get so busy working for God that they stop abiding with God.**

The warning signs of spiritual exhaustion are familiar:
* Irritability and impatience over minor issues.
* Cynicism and loss of joy in service.
* Performing spiritual duties mechanically while feeling distant from God inside.
* Craving unhealthy escapes or numbing distractions at the end of the day.

When we try to lead out of our own limited emotional and physical energy, burnout is only a matter of time.

---

## 2. Core Truth: Root Before Fruit

Jesus gave His disciples a simple metaphor for sustainable life and leadership:

> *"Remain in me, and I will remain in you. For a branch cannot produce fruit if it is severed from the vine, and you cannot be fruitful unless you remain in me. Yes, I am the vine; you are the branches... For apart from me you can do nothing."* (John 15:4–5)

Notice the order:
* **Connection comes before production:** A branch does not strain and sweat to produce grapes. It simply stays connected to the vine, absorbing life and nutrients, and fruit naturally follows.
* **Your inner life is your ceiling:** The depth of your private walk with Jesus will determine the long-term impact and health of your public leadership.
* **Rest is an act of trust:** Taking regular time for Sabbath rest, unhurried prayer, and Scripture reading is a declaration that God is in control of the world, not us.

When your soul is nourished by the presence of Christ, you lead out of overflow rather than exhaustion.

---

## 3. The 4 Areas of Growth for Module 6

* 🧠 **Head (Clear Thinking):** Leadership is a long-distance marathon, not a sprint. Soul care and spiritual rest are essential responsibilities, not guilty luxuries.
* ❤️ **Heart (Inner Character):** My primary identity is a beloved child of God, not my title, performance, or leadership role.
* ✋ **Hands (Practical Action):** Guard an unhurried, daily appointment with God (even 15 minutes) for Scripture reading, silence, and prayer.
* 🔁 **Habit (Abide to Grow):** Guard a daily unhurried appointment with Jesus (even 15 minutes), and spend 15 minutes every Sunday evening reviewing your week with God: *Where was God at work? What do I need to entrust to Him for the coming days?*
