# Facilitator Guide

**Group-session guidance for *How God Develops Leaders***

This guide contains the material for leading the six group meetings. Before each meeting, participants work through the corresponding course session, where teaching and reflection exercises are integrated in a **Learn, Reflect and Apply** sequence.

Each participant-session section has a reference such as **1.5** or **4.10**. Use the compact session map at the beginning of each facilitator session to direct the group to key material. Main discussion questions are identified as **DQ1**, **DQ2**, and so forth for easy verbal reference.

Use the suggested plans flexibly according to group size, available time, participant needs, and the pastoral context. Protect confidentiality, never pressure participants to disclose personal experiences, and follow appropriate safeguarding, organisational, pastoral, or professional processes when concerns extend beyond the scope of a group discussion.

## Session 1: Understanding How Your Story Shapes Your Leadership

### Group Aim

Help participants connect their formative experiences with present leadership strengths and vulnerabilities, without pressuring them to disclose private experiences or forcing a spiritual explanation for every event.

**Participant-session links:** [Open Session 1](01-session-1-sovereign-foundations.md) · [1.5 Map Your Leadership Story](01-session-1-sovereign-foundations.md#15-map-your-leadership-story) · [1.12 Reflect on Your Defining Commitment](01-session-1-sovereign-foundations.md#112-reflect-on-your-defining-commitment-to-god)

| Reference | Material | Group use |
|---|---|---|
| 1.2 | Sovereign Foundations | Key-concept review |
| 1.5 | Leadership-story map | Main reflection discussion |
| 1.12 | Defining commitment | Personal application |
| 1.15 | Choose what to share | Participant-led discussion |

### Before the Group Meets

Ask participants to:

1. Work through the session's teaching and embedded reflection exercises.
2. Select one or two responses they are comfortable discussing.
3. Bring one question or insight.

Prepare your own brief example containing one positive influence, one mixed influence, one strength, one vulnerability, and one way you are still growing. Avoid telling a story in which every difficulty has been neatly resolved.

### Suggested Group Plan (75 Minutes)

| Activity | Time |
|---|---:|
| Welcome, prayer, and check-in | 5 minutes |
| Review of key concepts | 10 minutes |
| Clarification questions | 10 minutes |
| Discussion of formative influences | 20 minutes |
| Discussion of commitment and surrender | 15 minutes |
| Application and prayer | 15 minutes |

### Open the Conversation

Ask each person to complete this sentence:

> One idea from the teaching that stayed with me was…

### Review the Key Ideas

Use questions rather than reteaching the whole lesson:

- What are Sovereign Foundations?
- How can the same experience produce both strength and vulnerability?
- What is a sovereign mindset?
- What does it not mean?
- What is a commitment boundary?

Summarise: Our early experiences may influence how we lead. A sovereign mindset helps us recognise God's redemptive work without denying harm. A commitment boundary is a meaningful response of faith, surrender, or availability to God.

### Discussion Questions (DQ1–DQ6)

Choose two or three:

- **DQ1.** What experience helped prepare you to influence or care for others before you held a leadership position?
- **DQ2.** What apparent disadvantage may have contributed to a present strength?
- **DQ3.** Which strength in your life also contains a possible vulnerability?
- **DQ4.** How has your culture or family background influenced your understanding of leadership?
- **DQ5.** What did you discover while completing your leadership timeline?
- **DQ6.** What is the difference between an emotional experience and a commitment that changes direction?

Helpful facilitator responses include:

- “What do you think that experience formed in you?”
- “How do you see that affecting your leadership today?”
- “What part of that strength do you want to retain?”
- “What part may need healing or maturity?”
- “What support would help you take the next step?”

Do not tell participants what God was doing in an event. Help them reflect and discern.

### Strengthen Each Participant's Next Step

Ask participants to review the next step they wrote. Partners may ask:

- Is the action specific?
- Is it realistic?
- How will you know it has been completed?
- Who can support you?
- Does it respond to something you genuinely learned?

### Closing Prayer

Pray for gratitude, wisdom, healing, humility, renewed surrender, and faithfulness in each participant's next step. No one should be pressured to disclose details before receiving prayer.

### After the Session: Facilitator Review

- Did participants understand the three main concepts?
- Did discussion move from theory to application?
- Was anyone pressured to share more than they wished?
- Did I interpret someone's story for them?
- Does anyone need appropriate pastoral or professional support?
- Were the next steps specific and realistic?

## Session 2: Building Character Before Influence

### Group Aim

Help participants understand character checks without producing shame, spiritual performance, or unsafe disclosure. Move from abstract agreement about character to one specific, accountable response.

**Participant-session links:** [Open Session 2](02-session-2-inner-life-growth.md) · [2.8 Reflect on Your Response Under Pressure](02-session-2-inner-life-growth.md#28-reflect-on-your-response-under-pressure) · [2.12 Repair and Accountability](02-session-2-inner-life-growth.md#212-repair-and-accountability)

| Reference | Material | Group use |
|---|---|---|
| 2.3 | Character checks | Key-concept review |
| 2.8 | Response under pressure | Main reflection discussion |
| 2.12 | Repair and accountability | Accountable application |
| 2.15 | Choose what to share | Participant-led discussion |

### Suggested Group Plan (75 Minutes)

| Activity | Time |
|---|---:|
| Welcome and review of last week's practice | 10 minutes |
| Concept review | 10 minutes |
| Case discussion | 15 minutes |
| Personal reflection discussion | 20 minutes |
| Application and accountability | 15 minutes |
| Prayer | 5 minutes |

### Review the Key Ideas

- Why is character foundational to influence?
- What are the three character checks?
- What is a healthy response to failure?
- When might repair require more than a private apology?

### Discuss a Character-Test Case

Present this situation:

> A new ministry leader reports higher attendance figures than actually occurred because the team has been under pressure to demonstrate growth. The leader says the difference is small and that the ministry needs encouragement.

Ask:

1. What is being revealed?
2. Who may be affected?
3. What truth applies?
4. What would faithful repair require?
5. What accountability could help prevent repetition?

### Discussion Questions (DQ1–DQ5)

Choose two or three:

- **DQ1.** Why can gifted leaders be tempted to neglect character?
- **DQ2.** Which is harder for you: admitting a mistake, waiting, receiving correction, or making repair?
- **DQ3.** How can a leader distinguish obedience to God from pressure imposed by another person?
- **DQ4.** What helps Scripture move from information to lived conviction?
- **DQ5.** How can a group practise accountability without becoming controlling?

### Safety and Accountability Notes

- Do not invite public disclosure of sexual sin, abuse, criminal conduct, or confidential safeguarding matters.
- Do not offer instant restoration to a role where trust has been seriously violated.
- Do not imply that confession removes consequences.
- Help participants identify appropriate pastoral, organisational, or professional support.
- Model grace and truth together.

### Confirm an Accountable Response

In pairs, participants state only the action and support they have chosen—not private details. Partners help make the action specific and arrange one check-in.

### After the Session: Facilitator Review

- Did participants distinguish conviction from shame?
- Did the session encourage accountability rather than secrecy?
- Did anyone disclose a matter requiring formal follow-up?
- Are next steps appropriate to the seriousness of the issue?

## Session 3: Learning Faithfulness Through Service

### Group Aim

Help participants view current responsibilities as developmental opportunities while resisting the unhealthy message that faithful leaders must accept every task or endure exploitation.

**Participant-session links:** [Open Session 3](03-session-3-ministry-tasks.md) · [3.10 Learn From a Recent Assignment](03-session-3-ministry-tasks.md#310-learn-from-a-recent-assignment) · [3.15 Choose One Next Step](03-session-3-ministry-tasks.md#315-choose-one-next-step)

| Reference | Material | Group use |
|---|---|---|
| 3.3 | Ministry task continuum | Key-concept review |
| 3.10 | Recent assignment | Main reflection discussion |
| 3.12 | Learning from disappointment | Optional deeper discussion |
| 3.15 | Choose one next step | Application and feedback |

### Suggested Group Plan (75 Minutes)

| Activity | Time |
|---|---:|
| Review of the daily integrity practice | 10 minutes |
| Concept review | 10 minutes |
| Task-mapping discussion | 20 minutes |
| Case study | 15 minutes |
| Application and feedback planning | 15 minutes |
| Prayer | 5 minutes |

### Review the Key Ideas

- What is a ministry task?
- What changes along the task continuum?
- What does the little–big principle teach?
- How can the principle be misused?
- Why is evaluation important?

### Questions for Understanding an Assignment

When a participant describes a task, ask:

1. What was actually entrusted to you?
2. Who benefits from the task?
3. What skill or character quality does it require?
4. What feedback is available?
5. Is the task appropriate to your authority and capacity?
6. What would faithfulness look like—not perfection, but faithfulness?

### Case Study

> A capable volunteer repeatedly accepts too many assignments. The volunteer completes visible teaching responsibilities but misses planning deadlines and leaves others to finish administrative work. The volunteer says, “My main gift is teaching; administration is not my calling.”

Ask:

1. What is this assignment revealing?
2. What is a gifting issue, and what is a faithfulness issue?
3. What responsibility should the volunteer accept?
4. What boundary or team support might be appropriate?
5. What would growth look like over the next month?

### Discussion Questions (DQ1–DQ5)

Choose two or three:

- **DQ1.** Which kinds of tasks do new leaders tend to undervalue?
- **DQ2.** When has an ordinary responsibility developed you?
- **DQ3.** How can leaders ask for feedback without becoming dependent on approval?
- **DQ4.** What is the difference between perseverance and unhealthy overcommitment?
- **DQ5.** How should a leader respond when an assignment appears mismatched with their gifts?

### Plan Feedback for One Assignment

Participants share one responsibility, one learning goal, and the person from whom they will request feedback. Encourage specific and sustainable commitments.

### After the Session: Facilitator Review

- Did participants connect tasks with development?
- Did anyone need permission to establish a healthy boundary?
- Did participants distinguish gifting from basic reliability?
- Are feedback plans specific and relationally appropriate?

## Session 4: Discovering and Developing Your Gifts

### Group Aim

Help participants move from gift labels to evidence, service, practice, community confirmation, and appropriate development.

**Participant-session links:** [Open Session 4](04-session-4-giftedness.md) · [4.10 What Evidence Do You Have?](04-session-4-giftedness.md#410-what-evidence-do-you-have) · [4.11 Test a Possible Gift Through Service](04-session-4-giftedness.md#411-test-a-possible-gift-through-service)

| Reference | Material | Group use |
|---|---|---|
| 4.2 | Giftedness set | Key-concept review |
| 4.10 | Evidence of giftedness | Main reflection discussion |
| 4.11 | Gift-development experiment | Application planning |
| 4.15 | Character required | Safety and maturity review |

### Suggested Group Plan (90 Minutes)

| Activity | Time |
|---|---:|
| Review of Session 3 feedback practice | 10 minutes |
| Concept review | 15 minutes |
| Giftedness-set exercise in groups of three | 25 minutes |
| Evidence and character discussion | 20 minutes |
| Development experiment planning | 15 minutes |
| Prayer | 5 minutes |

### Review the Key Ideas

- How do natural abilities, acquired skills, and spiritual gifts differ?
- What are gift-mix, gift-cluster, and focal element?
- What evidence helps confirm a possible gift?
- Why should new leaders avoid rushing toward labels?
- How are character and giftedness related?

### Explore Giftedness in Groups of Three

In groups of three, each participant has five minutes to describe:

- Two ministry experiences
- The kind of contribution they made
- The fruit they observed
- Feedback they have received

The listeners may offer observations using tentative language:

- “I notice a repeated ability to…”
- “The fruit you described may point toward…”
- “A supporting skill that could help is…”
- “I would like more evidence before concluding…”

Listeners should not declare another person's calling or assign a permanent label.

### Discussion Questions (DQ1–DQ5)

Choose two or three:

- **DQ1.** Why can gift labels become attractive to new leaders?
- **DQ2.** What is the difference between enjoyment and fruitfulness?
- **DQ3.** How can a church create safe opportunities for experimentation?
- **DQ4.** What happens when a leader uses giftedness to excuse poor teamwork?
- **DQ5.** What character quality is especially important for your possible gift?

### Guidance for Facilitators

- Respect the church's theology of spiritual gifts.
- Do not turn the session into a competitive ranking exercise.
- Ensure quieter, practical, and less public contributions are valued.
- Help participants distinguish lack of skill from lack of gift.
- Treat every giftedness conclusion as provisional.
- Keep the focus on serving people rather than constructing an identity.

### Review Each Gift-Development Experiment

Review each development experiment for clear beneficiaries, appropriate scope, feedback, and a review date. Help participants make experiments small enough to complete.

### After the Session: Facilitator Review

- Did participants base conclusions on evidence?
- Were public gifts valued more than quiet gifts?
- Did anyone need a different kind of ministry opportunity?
- Are development experiments safe, specific, and supervised?

## Session 5: Leading Through Conflict and Relationships

### Group Aim

Help participants respond to tension with humility, courage, truth, and accountability. Prevent misuse of the backlash concept as a way to dismiss criticism.

**Participant-session links:** [Open Session 5](05-session-5-relationships-and-authority.md) · [5.9 Reflect on a Recent Conflict](05-session-5-relationships-and-authority.md#59-reflect-on-a-recent-conflict) · [5.14 How Are You Using Authority?](05-session-5-relationships-and-authority.md#514-how-are-you-using-authority)

| Reference | Material | Group use |
|---|---|---|
| 5.5 | Leadership backlash cycle | Key-concept review |
| 5.9 | Recent conflict | Main reflection discussion |
| 5.14 | Use of authority | Accountability review |
| 5.17 | Choose one next step | Relational application |

### Suggested Group Plan (90 Minutes)

| Activity | Time |
|---|---:|
| Review of giftedness experiments | 10 minutes |
| Concept review | 15 minutes |
| Criticism discernment exercise | 20 minutes |
| Barnabas and Paul discussion | 15 minutes |
| Personal application in pairs | 20 minutes |
| Prayer | 10 minutes |

### Review the Key Ideas

- What is the submission cluster?
- How do position, trust, and spiritual authority differ?
- What are the stages of the backlash cycle?
- Why must the cycle remain a diagnostic rather than a defence?
- What does non-defensiveness include?

### Understand What the Criticism Is Saying

Read each statement and ask what kind of criticism it may represent and what further information is needed:

1. “You made this decision without speaking to the volunteers who must carry it out.”
2. “I do not like the new format; the old way felt more familiar.”
3. “You always ignore people like me.”
4. “The financial process you used did not follow our policy.”
5. “If you continue, I will make sure no one supports you.”

Emphasise that tone alone does not determine accuracy. Leaders must examine evidence, impact, process, and responsibility.

### Discussion Questions (DQ1–DQ5)

Choose two or three:

- **DQ1.** Why is criticism especially difficult for new leaders?
- **DQ2.** How can a leader remain teachable without becoming controlled by approval?
- **DQ3.** What does it look like to yield influence as Barnabas did?
- **DQ4.** When is documentation or formal review necessary?
- **DQ5.** How can leaders distinguish perseverance from stubbornness?

### Safety and Accountability Notes

- Do not mediate an active abuse or safeguarding case in the group.
- Do not invite participants to name people involved in confidential conflicts.
- Do not assume the person with the leadership title is correct.
- Do not recommend direct confrontation where there is danger or coercive control.
- Follow organisational reporting processes where required.

### Identify the Next Faithful Relational Step

Partners help one another identify the next faithful conversation, boundary, counsel, or formal process. They should not attempt to solve the entire conflict.

### After the Session: Facilitator Review

- Did participants understand that authority remains accountable?
- Did anyone use spiritual language to avoid ownership?
- Is any situation beyond the scope of group discussion?
- Are next steps safe, specific, and appropriately supported?

## Session 6: Leading From Spiritual Depth

### Group Aim

Help participants integrate learning from Phases I–IV into a realistic, supported development plan. Celebrate insight and obedience without suggesting that six sessions have produced mature leaders.

**Participant-session links:** [Open Session 6](06-session-6-life-maturing.md) · [6.13 Write Your Leadership Commitments](06-session-6-life-maturing.md#613-write-your-leadership-commitments) · [6.16 Create Your 90-Day Development Plan](06-session-6-life-maturing.md#616-create-your-90-day-development-plan)

| Reference | Material | Group use |
|---|---|---|
| 6.3 | Service flowing from spiritual depth | Main reflection discussion |
| 6.6 | Plateau check | Growth assessment |
| 6.13 | Leadership commitments | Values workshop |
| 6.16 | 90-day development plan | Final application and review |

### Suggested Group Plan (90 Minutes)

| Activity | Time |
|---|---:|
| Welcome and review of Session 5 practice | 10 minutes |
| Life Maturing concept review | 15 minutes |
| Plateau and challenge discussion | 15 minutes |
| Leadership values workshop | 15 minutes |
| 90-day plan review in pairs | 25 minutes |
| Commissioning prayer | 10 minutes |

### Review the Key Ideas

- What does the movement from doing toward being mean?
- What does it not mean?
- What are the four plateau warning signs?
- What kinds of challenge may expand capacity?
- Why should a new leader understand Life Maturing without claiming mature status?

### Discussion Questions (DQ1–DQ6)

Choose two or three:

- **DQ1.** Which achievement measures most easily become substitutes for formation?
- **DQ2.** How can spiritual disciplines become merely functional for leaders?
- **DQ3.** Which plateau sign is easiest to overlook?
- **DQ4.** How can we distinguish a growth challenge from an impulsive risk?
- **DQ5.** What helps a leadership value move from words to behaviour?
- **DQ6.** Which course insight most changed how you understand development?

### Strengthen Each Leadership Commitment

Ask participants to read one value in pairs. The listener checks:

- Is it clear?
- Is the strength word—must, ought to, or should—appropriate?
- Is the value consistent with Scripture and healthy leadership?
- Can it guide a real decision?
- What behaviour would make it visible?

Avoid turning personal preferences into universal commands.

### Review Each 90-Day Plan

Partners check whether each plan:

- Contains no more than five priorities
- Uses specific actions
- Includes support or feedback
- Defines observable evidence
- Includes a review date
- Respects health, family, work, and existing commitments
- Addresses character as well as skill

Encourage participants to remove goals that are impressive but unrealistic.

### Commissioning Prayer

Invite each participant to name one word or phrase describing the grace they seek for the next 90 days. Pray for faithfulness, humility, courage, wisdom, healthy relationships, and deeper communion with God.

### After the Course: Facilitator Follow-Up

- Contact participants after 30 days with a brief reflection prompt.
- Offer an optional group review after 60 days.
- Hold a 90-day review meeting or mentor conversation.
- Celebrate specific growth rather than visibility or promotion.
- Refer participants to further training appropriate to their responsibilities.
- Direct participants who wish to explore Phases V and VI to Clinton's original work.

### After the Session: Facilitator Review

- Did participants integrate learning from all four phases?
- Are plans realistic and supported?
- Did the group avoid equating course completion with maturity?
- Which participants need mentoring or additional support?
- How will follow-up be sustained after the course?
